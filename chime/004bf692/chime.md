If we probe the concepts and goals of religions we may generalize the concept to encompass also other belief systems that we tend not to call religions but that we should based on the similarities in how they are applied to adress the same issues of life.

1) A religion enables us to adress our groundlessness, mortality and insatiability (Ungers first characteristic of a religion). One may ground the view of reality by promoting faith in a Gods will (teisms), some method to find enlightenment (new age, Buddhism) or faith that rational thinking may bit by bit ground truth (science, humanism, atheism). Ungers suggests the religion of the future shall simply face our groundless view of reality as a fact, not shed it.  
2) A religion provides guidance on how to commit to life and a vision about our place in some ”grand plan” (Ungers second characteristic of religion). One may say that a God has such a plan for us or that God is part of such a plan (theism). Or one may say there is a hidden higher level of existence where we actually belong (new-age, buddism).  
3) A religion enables one to adress the incomprehensible complexity of the great questions about life (Ungers third characteristic of religion). Why are we here? What happens after death? What is the universe and why is it here? Religion provides tools to relate to them in a simplified way. One may say that a God created everything and knows why so we can just delegate this to God. Then the problem is simplified to follow Gods intentions and trust his will and we will be fine. One may also say that we should find peace as in stop asking these questions. Our goal is then to still ourselves in meditation or by focusing mindfully on the present. This is also a way to simplify the problem by trying to blind ourselves of the problem. Ungers suggests that the religion of the future should instead face these questions as forever unanswerable but still continuously investigable.

In this view of what religion is for we can identify more belief systems that we should treat also as religious beliefs.

a) Capitalism, liberalism and democracy. It is a religious belief because it simplifies life (3) by putting faith in ”the market” to magically provide for our needs, It grounds us by stating that our ”free will” will find the truth. It helps us not to think about our flaws (1) by telling us to focus on ourselves and our success in overcoming the struggles of life. It tells us how to commit to life (2) by telling us to ”succeed” as in menacing career, earn money, gather wealth and be admired and seen by others.  
b) Self-help communities and new-age ideas. It is a religious belief because it simplifies life (3) byt telling us all we have to do is find our inner strength and self instead of facing the complexities of reality. It tries to ground us by telling us the truth is actually within each of us if we just figure out how to listen. It tells us to overcome and rid ourselves from our flaws (1) by finding the inner strength to do so. It tells us about a grand-plan (2) that is not the one suggested by current society. We shall shield us from the destructive aspects of society, live with a self created bubble of protection made of serenity, inner strength and inner enlightenment.
