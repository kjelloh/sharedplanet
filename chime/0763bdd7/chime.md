I more and more gets convinced that there is something wrong with our praise of freedom that makes us blind to the conditions we have been evolved to interact (cope) with.

> *So the basic idea here is to use the metaphor of a "fly wheel" to identify processes in a human life that in fact is the core "thing" that drives it forward. If we act only on "free will" we will in fact get stuck in apathy. The basis for this conclusion is that the "will" we seem to be talking about is our urge to transform or not transform our reality (that is to act or not act). In this context "free" will mean that we have maximum option to act or not act as we feel we should. But then we have to ask what our "will" has been evolved to cope with. And to me it seems our urges are there to have us optimise our situation in relation to a hostile and scarce environment. We where never "free" to actually get what we "wanted". We could only strive towards that which we seemed too long for. And in doing so we ended up in some optimum relationship with our environment where we sufficiently got what we could get and avoided that which we did not want.*

> *So have we in the name of "freedom" replaced "truth" about our predicament with "will" to get what we want? As if what we feel we should do, as in avoid pain and strive for wellbeing, trumps what we in the past was compelled to content with. So much so that in our modern lives we no longer have the "fly wheel" of a harsh environment to be at the basis of our choice to act. Have we been fooled to trust "wellbeing" as the optimum state of life rather than finding the optimum balance in relationship with an unforgiving universe?*

> *To me it seems I need a "fly wheel" to drive my life forward when I do not. So I need my environment to expect me to deliver when I do not choose so myself. I need routines to get me through the boring necessities of making my bed, brushing my teeth, cleaning my apartment and brining food to my table. But I now suspect that is in fact not enough. I actually need a "fly wheel" external to my own control. I need to commit to living in an environment that requires me to interact with it is some clear and meaningful way. Like I need to commit to deliver services to other peoples. I need to commit to an environment where my choice of how to keep warm, how to be secure, how to get good sleep and ability to create is limited in a meaningful way. For me it seems the "freedom" to be able to do nothing and still get through the day is a kind of death. If I have the option to spend my whole day watching youtube videos and contemplating on philosophical questions I actually get anxious. I feel out of touch with reality. I feel out of control of my own situation. I suspect it may be the case that if I lived so that I needed to bring in wood and light a fire to keep my home warm I would find this meaningful and a way to keep me in touch and at the rudder of my journey through each day and through life.*

> *So I suspect the reason we experience we have something we call "free will" is that it is what we call our ability to add rational reasoning to how we care about our observed reality. In addition to most animals we are able to view our existence as separate from our body and mind. Our consciousness provides us with the experience to view ourselves and others from the "outside". And from that viewpoint reason about how to act in relation to how we model reality and how we care about our position to it. When we engage our "free will" we are in fact adding to what we already feel to be true. We add the dimension of creating a logical model of reality and rational reasoning within that model. In this way we can fine tune how to act that our "caring apparatus" was not able to find.*

> *From this we should suspect that for "free will" to have us interact with the unknown in the most "beneficial" way we need our reality to have a characteristic of a "fly wheel" drive. Our "will" is not in itself competent enough to serve as the main drive of our lives. The purpose of our "will" and the addition of rational "free will" is to "choose" our external "fly wheel" and interact with it in some regular and predictable way.*

> *And now we can also understand "will" as the truth about how to act generated by our unconscious self and "free will" as the rational reasoning addition that is "free" in the sense that it is able to question if what we feel to do is in fact the right thing to do, all things considered. When we say "free" we are in fact referring to ability to view ourselves as separated from ourselves. As conscious beings we are separated from reacting and put in a position with at least the option to rationally act. We are "free" to infer another reality than the one presented to us by our "caring apparatus".*
