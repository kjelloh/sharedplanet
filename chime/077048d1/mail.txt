It keeps making more and more sense to me to understand spoken language as "just" a representation of an abstraction of our internalised model of reality.

When we speak we always speak about something. This is so natural to us that stating this obvious thing does not seem to reveal any hidden truth about language. But I think it does. It reveals that language is to its nature "about" something. And we can propose that the "something" we are in fact representing with spoken language exists within us and start asking what that could be.

Now, to not get tangled up in a discussion on what exists and what is real I propose we instead use the terminology of models. A model being some abstraction that share traits with the thing it is a model of. So that the model is about the thing it models. And the model captures truths about the thing it models in an abstract form. And we can for now take a step back from having to ground our model of reality in insisting it models the true reality out there independent of what or who observes it.. We just settle with a model that shares traits with reality (as we observe it) when we interact with it. As if our model not necessarily captures what reality is but more how we experience it to behave. In a way we trust the model if it converges to stable state when we apply it in a closed loop when interacting with reality. As Sam Harris would say, whether we are living in a virtual reality or not it still is something for us to experience that is stable enough that we can make abstractions about it.

From this stance it now makes sense to say that language is "about" reality. The reality we observe. It is about the reality we experience. And it is about both the reality we experience to be "out there" as well as the reality that seems to exists within ourselves. So, for example, the confusion between the objective and the subjective now becomes part of the same model of reality. Just that the objective is the model of reality we experience to be external to ourselves while the subjective is about the part of the model of what goes on inside us.

So when we talk about a tree we are in fact representing something that is in our model of reality that we refer to using the word "tree". And this part of our model of reality that we give the label "tree" is much more rich and complex than just the object "tree", the living plant "tree" or the fruit supplier "tree". Depending on the context we are in fact referring and exchanging information about different parts of our model that is tied to the word "tree". If I say "I climbed the tree" I bring forward a complex model of reality that carries internalised information about how to interact with this tree-thing to elevate myself off the ground. While if I say "My dog peed on the tree" or "I sat down in the shadow of the tree" I am in fact bringing up two completely new and different aspect of how I may interact with the tree. To us we just experience it is the same tree we are talking about. When in fact we may use the word "tree" to bring up very different models or the reality we trust to explain how we interact with trees. This is important and shines a light on how we are able to utilise our understanding of things we model and label as "trees" when we are chased by a lion or a looking for honey. With language we can condense vast amount of information about our internalised understanding of the reality of "trees" with just a single word. Relying heavily on the trust the the one we are talking to brings the same model of "tree" into consciousness and so can follow my reasoning and implications.

The idea of spoken language as an abstraction of our model of reality enables us to stand between the extreme propositions that we observe and live in reality as it is and the more discouraging one that language creates reality. No, we interact with reality using an internalised model of reality that matches our experience of reality when we interact with it. Whatever is "out there" we live in a representation of it, a model of it, and language enables us to abstractly interact with each other about it. With language we can both cooperate using and exchange information about representations of reality. And where we can see other living creatures interact with reality using their internalised model of it, we seem to be able to operate on yet another layer of abstraction by being able to exchange models of reality using our language as an abstract representation of the representation of reality. Language is about about reality. Language is meta-meta-reality.
