I would like to measure the power I put into my bike.

1) Consider a shoe insole that can measure the downward force of the foot down through the shoe sole?

The basic idea here is to design a pressure sensitive insole with the ability to measure the total downward force of the foot down to the shoe.

This could be an alternative to having a pedal mounted power meter?

for this to work wee a insole design that is able to correctly enough measure the total downward force of the foot.

Now, I suspect the push will mostly go down through the middle of the front of the foot sole?  At least, that is how I imagine I crank my bike, having the shoe rest on the pedals at the a location slightly in front of the middle of the foot.

But I can be wrong. Maybe you are normally actually pressing down on the heel too?



2) Consider a shoe insole power meter can not measure the upward force when using clip-in pedals?

The basic assumption here is that when you use clip-in pedals you are able to pull the pedals up on the back stroke of the cranks and this will not be picked up by the pressure sensor of the insole?


3) Consider what design criteria we can imagine to have for our force measuring insole to work as part of a bicycle pedalling power meter?

I imagine the maximum downward force is limited by body weight and ability to accelerate or "jump" on the pedals?

If so, we can expect a maximum downward force to be between the riders weight (up to 100 kg or 1000N?) and 1 1G additional acceleration (to jump of the ground?) which gives us 2G or 200 kg and 2000N?

We can check these numbers by imaging a rider making 2 "jumps" of 100 cm each second. A maximum power output could then be say 2000N times 0,1m times two = 400J per second or 400W.

Well, it is in the ballpark so to say :)

Although I have heard that an elite cyclist can output up to 2000W?

If we imagine a human running uphill a 2000W output would mean an elevation of mgh = 2000J every second. This is an elevation of h = 2m every second for a 100kg heavy runner. That is a LOT!!

But again, actually in the ballpark of what I have seen elite skiers do on video?
