1) Consider to elaborate on a design where we utilise the lift force of a wing to drive a fly wheel?


Can we utilise the lift force of a wing to drive a fly wheel?

The main idea here is to utilise the lift force on a wing in the wind to drive a fly wheel and then an electric generator.

To make this work we have to add an additional mechanism so that we can change the wing attack angle when we re-position the wing into its low position for a new cycle. Either we do something with the orientation of the wing so that we do not have to loose energy fighting the wind when we lower the wing again. But even better would be to somehow position the wing so that in the down stroke the wind actually produces a down force. In effect driving the wing in both the up-stroke and down-stroke movement.

2) Consider if we can even design a "three phase" version of this flapping wing wind energy harvester?






From a one-wing design we should be able to build a "three phase" solution where three wings drive the fly wheel each 120 degrees offset relative each other. If we also have the wings operate on different heights we may be able to have each wing harvest its own layer of wind flow. Also, the angle offset may allow us to reduce the inertia requirements of the fly wheel as the three phase arrangement will by itself always drive the wheel in the same direction?
