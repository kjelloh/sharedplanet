2) Consider how neither the Left nor the Right experienced “pain” is actually adressed but rather the main focus is on fight and anger?

==&gt; Consider how the Cathy Newman interview can be framed both as “the left” being wrong about their pain from dysfunctional “order" as well as “the right” being wrong about the functional “order” of things?

> *Whilst the Cathy Newman interview with Jordan B Peterson has been framed as a “Glitch in the Matrix” where Peterson came through as the realist taking a firm stand on the grounds of observable truth. It is also possible to in that interview identify that Cathy Newman was trying to get Peterson to confirm that the Pain of having to compete in an unfair capitalist game is real. But Peterson dismissed any such feelings and Newman reacted with astonishment and not so well hidden contempt.*

> *The observation we can make is that whilst the Right (as in conservative protection of that-which-work) fights against the pain of seeing the kapitalist structure and supposed well working meritocracy being questioned and dismantled, the left (as in embracing progress and identifying things to enhance) is at the same time fighting against the pain of being victims to the unfairness of the same system. It seems both sides can’t be right at the same time? Or can they? Could it be that there is, in fact, a real common pain underpinning all major initiatives to preserve or enhance contemporary societies?*

> *Even I, as a western white middle aged privileged man feels tremendous pain when facing the prospect of continuing to play the game of excelling in becoming a good and valued Swede. It would mean I have to find some paid work on a job market where all work I can identify seems both boring and meaningless and at the same time also producing an outcome in society and the world being not only pointless but harmful even. I worked for many years as an IT consultant helping Swedish banks to state requirements for and certify card payment systems. A task that was to its abstract nature rewarding and intellectually intriguing, but as manifested in real systems and applications it did nothing to provide better or more meaningful life conditions to me or to those using it. And when I realised there was also no room to actually enhance the processes of performing the work I did, I finally just could not continue to waste my time however well paid the work I did was. In a way I just felt that the life I wanted to engage in could not be bought and then money got shifted out from the focus of what I wanted to aim for.*

> *It seems we can choose to trust that if humans feel pain, then this pain is real. And we have a choice to either adress or dismiss it. The interesting thing here is that both Jordan B Peterson, A psychological professor on the quest to adress the pain of having to live a meaningless and nihilistic life, as well as Slavoj Zizek, a psychoanalytical professor and philosopher that accuses kapitalism to rob humanity of meaning and value in work, seems to adress contemporary predicaments of human lives through the lense of Pain! They both identify the same pain. They just fight each other on what actions are required to deal with the Pain.*

> *While Peterson argues that Pain should be confronted and accepted, Lift up your cross and carry it uphill, Zizek confirms the feeling of victimhood and that the Pain is a signal that the crosses we are supposed to carry are the wrong ones. But they both agree that we have a problem of Pain however materialistic content the modern human has become.*

> *EVen Sam Harris, swinging Occam´s razor left and right to leave only the naturalistic observable existence as worth cultivating, agrees that Pain is the most real thing in human lives. But also he firmly takes sides, in his case against populism and it's call to preserve traditional proven structure of order and demands humanity to embrace change and progress on the basis of it being the only true enlightened and reasonable way to struggle with Pain.*
