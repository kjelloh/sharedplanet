Watching a one hour lecture by German Philosopher Markus Garbriel (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wX1YMMKuSgs) and a review of his book ”Why the World does not Exist” by the Guardian (http://www.theguardian.com/books/2015/oct/30/why-world-does-not-exist-markus-gabriel-review) I come think it seems he proposes the same view on ”reality” as theoretical physics, namely that we live in one of many parallell possibilities.

1) Gabriel seem to take a position between Realism (there is only one reality) and post modernism (there are infinite constructed realities) in that there is in fact an infinite domains of perspectives but each having a limited set of ”truths” (true or false believes)?

”The universe as understood by Gabriel, then, is not only a thing out there but also a kind of perspective, the perspective of science. By contrast, the world contains all ontological perspectives and, given that there are in principle an infinite number of such perspectives, that means it is pretty big. And the world is nonexistent because we can never have a perspective on it from the outside. As the great philosopher and Gabriel’s former teacher Thomas Nagel put it, there is no view from nowhere.”

2) This seem to correspond quite well with contemporary theoretical physics where we live in one of infinite number of possible realities (universes) each having a limited set of ”truths” (laws of nature) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiverse)?

”The multiverse (or meta-universe) is the hypothetical set of finite or infinite possible universes, including the universe we live in. Together, these universes comprise everything that exists: the entirety of space, time, matter, energy, and the physical laws and constants that describe them.”

3) Is this not the same approach to the problem of being unable to ground knowledge? When faced with being unable to answer ”what” reality is, ”if” it is, then we surrender by stating that ”many” possibilities exists and that we choose which one to relate with (apply an aspect as put by Gabriel) or just happen by chance to live in this particular universe (multiverse theory)?

4) But is this not a contradiction to the goal of reasoning in the first place, that which tries to say something about reality that is simpler that reality itself? Is not the goal of philosophy, science and religion to find ”the answer”? Could we not then state that proposing a ”multi answer” kind of fall short in that conquest?

5) Unger and Smolin argues against this proposition of multiverse precisely on this point that for an answer to have a meaning it must reduce the complexity of reality? Should not philosophy argue that a philosophical relation with reality should aim for the One Asnwer and not settle with a Multianswer?
