In search for how to make sense of the contemporary human predicaments and collective reactions to them it seems to me that there is a "How Dare You" reaction that we can identify behind many of them?

The idea here is to spot the "How Dare You" reaction in as many of the collective reactions in contemporary societies as we can.

I got the idea while watching Youtuber Jellybean Gen video "The last scandals of 2019" commented on the failures of Swedish Politicians as "spitting in the eyes" of Swedish Citizens. To me this signalled an emotion of not showing respect to what the people want. And the think she wanted was to be shown respect. She wanted to live in a community where she could feel respected, safe and proud. Or at least that would be the words I would use to describe what she wanted politicians to provide. One way to summarise her dismay with how politicians are responsible for Sweden not being the country she like it to be seems very close to "How Dare they"!

I mean, how similar is this not to Gretha Thunberg accusing the UN to have failed in a very similar way? How dare they destroy her future? How dare they violate what she values and hold precious? Just as Jellybean Gen holds Swedish Politicians responsible for having ruined Sweden. How dare they do this?

And now we can identify the exact same set of emotions and reactions in the #metoo movement. Women rightfully demanding respect and safety in the workplace and in their homes. How dare all men allow the lives and security and respect of women to be violated? How Dare you?!

So the common theme here seem to be about security, protection and respect. The importance to be valued as a worthy member of the collective. To be allowed to interact on equeal terms. To be part of the creative process to form the future. To be relevant in any discussion about what is right and wrong and to be treated as someone to listen too!

Because this seems to be at the core of what Donald Trum is talking about when he says "Make America Great Again". Something important is being violated that is worthy of respect and to be listened to. It seems to be at the core of Brexit. Those in power neglects the need of its citizens. Those in power violates the value of feeling proud and secure in ones own country. It seems to be at the core of Swedish Populism under the flag of the Swedish Democratic Party.  Those in power has violated the values of taking care of our elderly; Giving away our social security system without sufficient restrictions to immigrants that has not earned the right to it at the cost of us living here that has payed for it. How dare they?!!

In this analysis we can go as far as saying that the most pure form of this cry for respect and justice and dismay to those violating it is the so called Social Justice Warrior movement. Just with the twist that here the cry is to extend the right for respect, safety and to be listened too to everyone that feels they are in the position of being neglected. Only that in being a movement to change society into something it has never been, in effect demanding the existing society to be teared down to make place for the one we all rightfully deserves, the "How Dare You" becomes much more abstract. For an outsider the Cancel Culture, transactivism, latest wave of feminism, neo marxism and the ever growing spectrum of self identified genders and their right for expression and respect can seem overwhelming. But can we not see the "How Dare You?!" cry in the argumentation towards classical conservative ideas? The progressive left accuses the conservative right to violate oppressed minorities when defending that which has been shown to "Work". How dare they?!! The response is an accusation that the progress the left is requiring will dismantle precious values whilst refusing to show these values respect. How Dare They?!

Well, maybe there is a limit to how far we can run with this model of "How Dare You" mentality? Never the less the pattern is clear enough to use as a way to analyse and apply as a model of what is going on to allow for rational reasoning. Where do we meet. Is there a way to understand that at some basic level we are all driven by the same demand to be taken seriously and not be dismissed or shut down. We all want respect and security to be able to find our way to a good life? We care! We all care! We just have to find a way to care together.

David Hume has famously stated "In every system of morality, which I have hitherto met with, I have always remark'd, that the author proceeds for some time in the ordinary way of reasoning, and establishes the being of a God, or makes observations concerning human affairs; when of a sudden I am surpriz'd to find, that instead of the usual copulations of propositions is, and is not, I meet with no proposition that is not connected with an ought, or an ought not.". We have understood this as the problem of getting and "ought" form an "Is". Maybe it is time to realise that there is in fact a way to get an "Ought" from an "Is". The only thing you have to do is to place a living being into the scene. This being will be unable to NOT produce an "Ought". In fact, it is at the very core of being alive, not only a human but being alive in any form, to assess what "is" and come up with how it "ought" to be and then try to act accordingly. Simply put, we care! And until, if ever, we become able to somehow compute the "ought" trough some invention that does NOT involve a human being, we have to come to terms with how we "ought" to care together as a society!

Maybe we can observe every "How Dare You" as one of our deepest caring about how others are treating us wrongly. And from there go on to propose that however important and valid part of our caring machinery the "How Dare You" reaction is, it is only one piece of the puzzle. From there we need to device a collectively acceptable and reasonable viable action. And this action must almost by definition be based on respect, honesty and inclusivity whilst at the same time paradoxically proceed by establishing some common right and wrong value system. ANd in doing so will have to question our sacred "Freedom" in the process. The paradox is of course that for something to be right something else has to be wrong which a all encompassing freedom will be unable to reconcile.
