Smolin makes a strong case of explaining the flaws of Newtonian sub-system law finding approach when it comes to finding laws that governs a complete system, i.e. the Universe.

His simply reasons (In ”The Singular Universe and Reality of Time” chapter ”THE FAILURE OF THE NEWTONIAN PARADIGM WHEN APPLIED TO COSMOLOGY”) that Newtonian paradigm requires multiple copies of isolated sub-systems applicable to variations in initial conditions. The laws are expected to be invariant to time and initial conditions and will thus reveal themselves in the variants of the system end states. But this approach will fail when we scale up the sub-system to cover the complete universe. The universe can’t be copied into several systems and it can’t be subject to varying initial conditions (there is nothing external).

This reasoning is strikingly hard to oppose. Further, it is not restricted to be applied to physical systems only.

What can we say about the ”laws” of human society using Smolin way of reasoning from sub-systems to complete systems? If the whole human society is a complete system and occurrences like families, fan-clubs, cooperations, companies, countries are sub-systems within it, then what conclusion about the whole human society may we draw from studying our behavior in these sub-systems?
