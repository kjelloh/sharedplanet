	==> Consider Intels page on Thunderbolt (https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/io/thunderbolt/thunderbolt-technology-developer.html <https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/io/thunderbolt/thunderbolt-technology-developer.html> )?

	==> Consider Intels technical brief on the Thunderbolt 3 technology (https://thunderbolttechnology.net/sites/default/files/HBD16235_Thunderbolt_TB_r05.pdf <https://thunderbolttechnology.net/sites/default/files/HBD16235_Thunderbolt_TB_r05.pdf> )?

		==> It seems Thunderbolt provides a physical layer capable of switching operation mode to act as USB, DisplayPort or Thunderbolt protocol transfers (where Thunderbolt mode in itself can transfer a combination of DisplayPort and PCIe on the available four channels of data)?

“Fundamentally, Thunderbolt is a tunneling architecture designed to take a few underlying protocols and combine them onto a single interface. That way, the total speed and performance of the link can be shared between the underlying usages of these protocols – whether they are data, display, or something else.

At the physical interface level, Intel’s Thunderbolt 3 silicon builds in a few important features:

	• A physical interface (PHY) layer that can dynamically switch its operating mode to drive either:

- USB 2.0, 3.0, and 3.1
- DisplayPort* 1.1 and 1.2a
- Thunderbolt at 20 Gbps and 40 Gbps

	• In the Thunderbolt mode, the Thunderbolt 3 port has the ability to support at least one or two (4-lane) DisplayPort interface(s), and up to four lanes of PCI Express Gen 3."

	==> So it would seem Thunderbolt is in itself NOT a protocol but a tunnel technology for DisplayPort, USB and PCIe?

		
		Thunderbolt four physical lanes can be used as two bi-directional data lanes (https://thunderbolttechnology.net/sites/default/files/HBD16235_Thunderbolt_TB_r05.pdf <https://thunderbolttechnology.net/sites/default/files/HBD16235_Thunderbolt_TB_r05.pdf> )

4) Consider good resources on the DisplayPort (DP) Protocol?

	==> Consider wikipedia on the DisplayPort (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DisplayPort <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DisplayPort> )?

		==> It seems a displayport connection consists of a a set of unidirectional and differential lanes where standard is four lanes?

“The DisplayPort main link is used for transmission of video and audio. The main link consists of a number of unidirectional serial data channels which operate concurrently, called lanes. A standard DisplayPort connection has 4 lanes," 

		==> It seams each lane in the DisplayPort main link can operate in a set of modes with different speed and characteristics?

“DisplayPort's transmission modes are as follows:

	• RBR (Reduced Bit Rate): 1.62 Gbit/s bandwidth per lane (162 MHz link symbol rate)
	• HBR (High Bit Rate): 2.70 Gbit/s bandwidth per lane (270 MHz link symbol rate)
	• HBR2 (High Bit Rate 2): 5.40 Gbit/s bandwidth per lane (540 MHz link symbol rate), introduced in DP 1.2
	• HBR3 (High Bit Rate 3): 8.10 Gbit/s bandwidth per lane (810 MHz link symbol rate), introduced in DP 1.3”

“The total bandwidth of the main link in a standard 4-lane connection is the aggregate of all lanes:

	• RBR: 4 × 1.62 Gbit/s = 6.48 Gbit/s bandwidth (data rate of 5.184 Gbit/s or 648 MB/s with 8b/10b encoding)
	• HBR: 4 × 2.70 Gbit/s = 10.80 Gbit/s bandwidth (data rate of 8.64 Gbit/s or 1.08 GB/s)
	• HBR2: 4 × 5.40 Gbit/s = 21.60 Gbit/s bandwidth (data rate of 17.28 Gbit/s or 2.16 GB/s)
	• HBR3: 4 × 8.10 Gbit/s = 32.40 Gbit/s bandwidth (data rate of 25.92 Gbit/s or 3.24 GB/s)"

6) Consider good resources to understand the 8b/10b encoding that many (all?) contemporary transfer interfaces seem to use?

	==> Consider this article from embedded.com <http://embedded.com/> on 8b/10b Encoding (https://www.embedded.com/design/debug-and-optimization/4371977/Troubleshoot-and-verify-8b-10b-encoded-signals-with-a-real-time-oscilloscope <https://www.embedded.com/design/debug-and-optimization/4371977/Troubleshoot-and-verify-8b-10b-encoded-signals-with-a-real-time-oscilloscope> )?

		==> It seems DisplayPort, PCIe and USB all uses 8b/10b Encoding?

“Few serial technologies have become more widely adopted than 8b/10b coding, which is now used in standards like PCI-Express, Serial ATA, SAS, Fibre Channel, InfiniBand, FireWire, MIPI M-PHY, HDMI, DisplayPort, CIPRI, OBSAI, XAUI, USB3.0 and others."

		==> Note: It says nothing about Thunderbolt itself using 8b/10b encoding (but maybe that comes without saying to tunnel DisplayPort, USB and PCIe)?

		==> It seems 8b/10b encoding is to ensure a nearly 50/50 distribution between transferred ones and zeroes (to avoid DC drift of the signal)?

“Each 8 bit word corresponds to two 10b characters to ensure the long term ratio between 1´s and 0´s is nearly 50 percent,” 

		
		The 8b/10b encoding keeps the DC drift low by ensuring 50/50 relation between transferred zeroes and ones (https://m.eet.com/media/1158451/tek8b-10bfig2.jpg <https://m.eet.com/media/1158451/tek8b-10bfig2.jpg> )
