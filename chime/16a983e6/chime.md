> *Somehow I get the intuition that while psychoanalysis claims to "see" the hidden, the irrational, the perversion and the so be able to reveal the "sick" hidden in human drives and activities, it still fails to drive the insight all the way home? At the end of the day humans acts to transform reality into what it "ought to be". And at the very least we should be able to claim that whatever those drives are, they come from abilities within ourselves to build a vision of an alternative, a transformed future state of reality, that has survived the test of time. And so far I have not seen Psychoanalysis take its observations and models of human behaviour to that point. To me it seems Psychoanalysis, and also post structuralism, has gotten stuck in the shamanic joy of having revealed hidden truths about the human mind. They happily tell these stories and examples of how human actions seem to be based on truth when in fact the objective does not exist, and vice versa.*

> *Zizek and Rollins repeatedly tells the story about the jealous husband convinced his wife is cheating on him. And that the bases of his suspicions can remain a lie even after the fact that the wife his been discovered to actually cheat on her husband. The reason for the husband to suspect his wife was not there. He had no actual reason to suspect his wife because the signs of his wife cheating on him was not available to him. It was is fantasy that lied to him and the fact that his suspicions about his wife actions was correct, the reason for him to draw that conclusion was not. Now they tell this story with a devotion as if there is something profound to learn from this. Zizek for example uses this story to analyse how Jordan B Petersson is going after the far left. I get it that Zizeks point is that Peterson acts out a drive to find an enemy onto he can project his fear of society falling apart. Peterson needs an antagonist to justify his fear and anger. But that Peterson is in fact wrong about the reason he projects his feelings onto the far left. Still, Zizek agrees the far left should be argued against. That they do pose a threat to society. By that Zizek is more true about why the far left has to be fought because Zizek does "not need" the far left as an antagonist. He bases his reason on truth while Peterson bases his argument on anxiety? He also seems to argue that the anxiety of the far left is true, although the far left itself acts in a perverted manner in respinse to this anxiety?*
