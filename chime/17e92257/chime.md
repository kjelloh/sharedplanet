So, how much does our behaviour really depend on us having a consciousness?

1) We expect to be the only animal having a consciousness? But still, animals are capable of performing quite complex tasks (e.g. birds, octopus and dolphins solving problems that seem to involve reasoning). So, would we still be able to reason and solve problems if we were not self.-aware?  
2) We are capable to automate quite complex tasks as long as they are routine (e.g. drive a car to work or home whilst thinking of complete other things). So, if we lost our self-awareness, would we still be able to perform such complex tasks?  
3) We seem to have a System 1 (unconscious pattern recognition) and System 2 (reflective, questioning and reason) as mechanisms of the brain. Question is, how much of System 2 is part of our consciousness? Could we propose that even a large part of System 2 would continue to work for us also if we loose our inner observer?  
4) If it is our self-awareness that enables us to cooperate flexibly enlarge groups (trust others to act-in-faith to a common idea), then do we loose this ability if we loose self-awareness?  
5) Consider to use the output of this reasoning to propose to what degree the “illusion of self“ may be drawn? To what degree can our self be an illusion? How log may we assume our self-control and self-awareness really is?
