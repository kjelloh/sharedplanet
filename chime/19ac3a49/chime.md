We seem to feel if characters or events in fiction (e.g. vary tales, science function, fantasy) are true to the ”set of truths” in that context or if the does not make sense. Can we argue that this ability of ours is actually at play also in our normal day-to-day lives?

1) In Harry Potter they can do everything with magic - but they don’t. Sometimes they cleans up a messy house with magic. Other times they clean themselves. But for some reason it ”makes sense” as if there are rules we do not know about that they follow and it makes sens to us.  
2) James Bond always survives and is able to pull of tricks with gadgets that simply would not work in real life (to unprovable or simply physical impossible). But in the ”world” of James Bond it makes sense. And we kind of know what is ”true” and what is ”not true” in the world of James Bond.  
3) In Transformers they are able to fold themselves in and out of the body of wheeled vehicles with combustion engines. And although we do not understand where the ”car and truck stuff” goes when they are robots we buy into the concept. It seems to be a set of rules they obey that is kind of coherent.  
4) Thus. we seem to be able to buy into and ”sense” a coherent set of rules although we do not know these rules per se. And we do not seem to need to know them. We kind of ”feel” they are there and that they are ”obeyed”.  
5) The opposite is also true. We are able to feel when the characters or events does not ”obey” the set of rules they are supposed to. That is, we seem to be able to sense when the rules are incoherent.  
6) Can we argue that this ability of ours to sense truth-web-coherence is actually at play also in our normal day-to-day lives?  
7) I.e., is out truth web actually carrying only truths that goes ”well together” rather than ”actually true”?  
8) Could we argue that once we selected our set of ”ungrounded truths” (those where we have no actual information about whether they are true ot not), then we selectively believe in new truths only if they ”rime” with the truths we already trust?
