Some thinkers talk about the benefits of having a currency that you can’t save but is incited to spend before its value is lost.

The benefit of such a currency is that it creates a force against the ”triple up” effect where the rich can continue to pile up richness in which case it is not spent on the market and thus won’t benefit others. Having a currency that decays in value would mean created value is kept circulating in the economy.

One argument for this currency is that it mimics nature. Sure you can gather value for some time (as in gathering food for coming not so abundant times) but the gathered value decays and is consumed by other organisms like bacteria. Thus, all value created in nature (food, energy, physical strength, even life itself) always naturally decays into a lower valued or unexacting state over time. That evolution has not come up with anything that behaves otherwise is one argument that eternally stored value is not a good idea?

Roman (or was it greek) cities applied a kind of this system where each citizen could check out a defined amour of seed each day. This was given free and the citizen may use it as seen fit. Bakeries accepted seed and baked bread on behalf of citizens. I suppose bakers then was paid in seed to or if they also and a system of money on the side? At the very least I suspect seed to could be traded for money and vice versa so the seed handed out worked as a kind of ”basic income”.
