So, can we identify the gap between all the "factually true" models of reality and the smaller set of applicable ones that gives us good "traction" and "direction" in our individual and collective act-in-faith though life?

1) Consider to compare Jordan B Peterson and Sam Harris model of reality in regards to its ability to give us "traction" and indicate "direction" executable by our ought-producing drives?

> *So the idea here is that what Sam Harris and Jordan B Peterson is doing is not so much talk about what reality is. Rather they are promoting two different models of reality. Where Peterson is reverse engineering his model from cultural and religious texts and practices that are still popular today and has stand the test of time. Sam Harris comes from the other direction and proposes the model we have built to interact with the material universe, the natural science model of the world. Now these two models are quite different in that they seem incompatible. For one, the traditional religious model of reality clashes in the details with almost everything the natural science model of the world tells us is true. But on the other hand, Peterson argues for a model of reality that includes things that humans care about. With emphasis on the caring part. And this I think is a crucial observation. As humans we care. And the things we care about often seems irrational to us. Or at the very least the caring just springs into existence as some self evident thing. And it seems to have very little to do with the things nature "care" about as modelled by the natural sciences.*

> *To build some intuition into how the natural science model of reality and the, let's say, spiritually embellished ones. With the natural science model of reality we can predict where nature "cares" for stuff to be or to go. Given the initial conditions os some part of reality, say a ball hit with a bat, we can predict where nature says the ball ought to go. By using "ought" in this manner we can frame the natural science model of reality as one that models where nature stipulates stuff ought to be given how reality is right now. Yes, using "ought" with this specific meaning we can say we now how nature transform an "is" to an "ought". We can encode this using the natural laws of physics.*

> *Now place a human in front of the ball that has just been hit by a bat. To predict how the human thinks she ought to interact with the ball is a totally other matter. We can do this. But only if we are human beings that understands something about the human we are trying to model and the game that human is playing. In this case we are of course talking about baseball. Or we could be talking about the Swedish game brännboll. The thing to observe is that in the reality a human experience, there are actors that act to transform reality into what it "ought" to be that we can not predict using the natural sciences. And we can talk as much as we want that we "ought" to be able to do this. Given that humans seems to be implemented in the stuff we can explain by the natural sciences. Still, these models have yet to be applicable for predicting human behaviour and they have yet to be executable by human brains in the day to day life.*

> *My argument is that, as humans, we need a model of reality to reason about stuff we care about. Caring about stuff can be a way to observe how we transform reality from an is to an ought. So when Hume observed and formulated the fact-value-gap as "...and let us see, that the distinction of vice and virtue is not founded merely on the relations of objects, nor is perceived by reason" it is easy to rephrase this as "you can't get an is from an ought". But then we forget that any human, in fact every living thing, can do this. To be alive is to act in reality driven by what you experience reality to be and then deduce how you should act to transform it into how it should be.*

> *Harris has observed this and suggested a model of reality that includes, or is based on, his vision of a moral landscape. In this moral landscape hills are models of reality where things are "good" and valleys are models of reality with more suffering. So if we could find a model of reality that can reveal these hills and valleys in human experience of the world we could also get to a model of reality we can use to agree on the "best" way top transform how reality "is" into what it "ought" to be.*

> *And framed this way Peterson has the same aim to prose the model he claims to have reversed engineered from evolved culture and religion. Only he is more forgiving for his model carrying arguments for what "truth" to act-in-faith to than Harris is. Harris bases his argument on the axiom that you should avoid acting in faith to models of reality that is, or seems to be, in contradiction with the natural model of reality. While Peterson stacks up a value hierarchy where you should trust the model that produces the "best" life rather than the model that best predicts how nature behaves.*

> *Maybe this could be best seen in the discussion between Peterson and Matt Dillahunty? Anything that has no observable and repeatable cause-and-effect correlation or is not predicted to have such a correlation by a scientifically accepted model of reality is just off the table. While Peterson, especially in this discussion, argues from the "what works" based on how "it feels" to those acting-in-faith to more non scientific models of reality. Peterson in fact was arguing that he preferred the model of reality that gave his patients better "traction" and better "aim" that what current scientific model could provide. And he was willing to dispatch to future scientists to formulate the actual acceptable model that could represent the "truth" behind these practices.*

> *For now I lean more to Peterson than Harris when it comes to argue what model of reality we should trust. As a physicist I trust the natural model of reality with my life. But I also observe that this model is of little or no use when I want to transform reality into one where the things I care about come true. I want to spend time doing meaningful things. I want to be loved. I want to love. I want to live as free of anxious as I can. I do not want to suffer more than I choose to or may convince myself I need to. And for that I need a more tolerant model of reality that I can also make myself believe and trust; Especially in absence of factual proofs. I need to trust other people and I need to trust things I do at least to some degree "works" although I am not fully sure. And for me the observation that human actors driven by "caring" interacting in our society we live in a reality of a complex self-organising system where many of the facts are like leafs in the wind depending on how others care about what others care about. The stories we tell about what is "true", about how we "should" act and how we should or should not care about stuff, these stories is locked in by what we observe. We tell stories to model the experienced reality of human life. And we are together searching for the story that explains the most about the stuff we care about and that fits what we observe and how we care about things to feel. And that trumps the so far much narrower model of how nature care matter and energy should move around.*

> *Finally, the problem with religion is not that it can make good people do bad things. It is not even that stories about reality we call religion clashes with the natural model of reality. The problem is that we now live with the illusion that we can navigate through a human life without betting on things to be true although we do not actually know. Our ability to interact with the unknown is in this way reduced. Which in turn shows up in how human societies gets more and more fractioned into camps of competing truths, sometimes only as a protest to those who claims they have the "best" truth. But we can't escape that we so far have no clue how to have our caring black-box inside us produce actions that are good for us as individuals and for the society as a whole. But for sure, we need to allow people to care for different things. But we also need some understanding of human caring so that we can start converging on what to care about. And how to care for those who cares for the wrong thing. And this is what we call a religion. Humans ability to model reality to efficiently interact with the unknown result of our collective interaction with the unknown is our super power. This flexibility has made us spread to every corner of the earth. This is what traditional religions has provided a model of. This is what enlightenment and science is trying top replace, making it just an updated religion. And this is why what we are looking for is a new religion in the sense of a model of reality that we can believe and trust, one that does not clash with the model of matter and energy and one which can make humanity act-in-faith to converge to a emergent social behaviour with better traction and better aim?*
