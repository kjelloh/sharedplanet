Watching Stephen Woodford (youtube channel Rationality Rules) vs. Carl Benjamin (Youtube Sargon of Akkad) video "The Left, the Right, and the Milkshake | Stephen Woodford vs. Carl Benjamin (Sargon of Akkad)" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9MijWxcKa3Y&t=2700s) arguing the value or correctness of nut picking as a tool to critique a political movement it suddenly seemed to me that Benjamin argued that  "the nuts" are "the teeth" of a movement while Stephen argued they should be regarded as "not representative"?

So my inferred metaphor is that when you see someone "nut picking" as observing "the nuts" of some political movement, ideology or religion you may ask yourself if this is a way to identify "the teeth" of the thing "biting you" or if you should view them as not actually part of the social movement you are trying to understand or describe.

I actually think Benjamin made a good point in stating that the persons he picks out as embedded in "the left" are those "with teeth" in that they are prominent either on social media or in political power. As such they have influence wether or not those identifying as "left" hold the same extreme views. So Stephens critique that they are not representative at least falls from the perspective that this is of less importance if they still transform society in ways you want to oppose. So if "the left" allows "the nuts" to act on there behalf as "the teeth" to bite current social structures they are representative of the change you want to oppose.

So how can we reconcile both Stephens critique and Benjamins defence to hold true? Well one way to model their discussion is that they are at one level discussing how to infer "the correct" model of reality. In this youtube video they get stuck on who is "right" when they could have identified the sub discussion on how to understand reality. Behind, What is "the left", what is "a woman", what is the right and so on lies the question of - How do we infer a model of reality where these "things" exist.

So my argument here is that to be able to rationally reason about anything you need a model that is as isomorphic as possible to the reality you are reasoning about. And for one the process of identifying these models are NOT about deduction; It is about wise observations followed by wise pruning with say Occam's razor. We should also be honest about how what we care for plays a large part in what we observe and how we prune our models of reality. It is NOT a crime to admit that intuition and caring is the framing of the whole process of reasoning! I think that is the secret ingredient I would like to bring to all rational discussions.

So in a way we can view Stephens Critique and Benjamins defence as coming from "caring" about different aspects of the discourse. Stephen is concerned  (cares for) about addressing the majority of those voting for "the left" while Benjamin is concerned (cares for) the part of "the left" that hearts what he want to protect in society. And both can be said to be "correct" to care for these two aspects of what "the left" is about. You can safely say that it is "correct" to interact with "the left" in these two ways at the same time. And that Stephen and Benjamin could have done, and did not, is to identify this meta level of the discussion and made it a separate one. That is, the meta discussion could have been based on wether what they "care" for are relevant or could be related to one another to find if one is more "valuable". And from that separate out what is right and wrong about their model of reality.

So, are "the nuts" of "the left" in fact "the teeth" of the movement? And if so, should "the left" allow them to be? And if not, how should "the left" get new more appropriate "teeth"?
