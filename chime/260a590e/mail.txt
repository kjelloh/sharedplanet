What is 'IFC' in BIM for House Building Projects?
IFC (Industry Foundation Classes) is an open, standardized file format used in Building Information Modeling (BIM). It enables seamless data exchange between different BIM software applications. For house-building projects, IFC ensures that architects, engineers, and contractors can collaborate efficiently, regardless of the BIM software they use.

IFC supports various aspects of a construction project, including:

3D geometry (walls, floors, roofs, etc.)
Material properties
Structural and MEP (Mechanical, Electrical, and Plumbing) data
Project metadata (cost, scheduling, sustainability)
It is developed and maintained by buildingSMART International and is widely adopted in the AEC (Architecture, Engineering, and Construction) industry.

What is the Swedish Counterpart for 'IFC'?
In Sweden, IFC is still the standard for BIM data exchange and is widely used in construction projects. However, Sweden has additional BIM-related frameworks and guidelines, including:

CoClass – A classification system developed by Svensk Byggtjänst for structuring BIM data.
BSAB – A Swedish construction classification system used for structuring building components, which works alongside IFC.
BIM Alliance Sweden – A Swedish organization promoting BIM standards and interoperability, including IFC usage.
Thus, there is no direct Swedish "counterpart" for IFC, as IFC itself is an international standard also used in Sweden. However, Swedish classification systems like CoClass and BSAB work alongside IFC to enhance BIM workflows in Sweden."
