I want a model of human activity and experience that enables me to reason about how to live my life in concert with all others.

> *So back again to the idea of humans as conscious ants that can "program" how we act together to have new "social objects" emerge into existence. Maybe we can come close to describe "who" we are and what "reality" we live in by stating - We are "programmers" as in we are able to change our "software" that affects how we interact with reality. As actors we are members of social objects that behaves as self-locking complex systems. Small changes in our "software" causes us to model reality in new ways and in effect "see" new things and act in new ways. If we base our model of our actions as driven by our ability to "care" we can propose that the thing we "program" in the end is "how we care". We are "care driven" actors. From this we can propose we have evolved with a "care producing processor" with an limited set of emotions to signal how we care. But we can expand our definition of "care" as any relation between our experienced reality and our action in response to this reality. So from the reflex to withdraw a hand from the hot stove all the way up to our passionate love for another human is at its core a "care driven" response. Only the first one seems hardware implemented and the latter at least in part software transformable (Freud anyone?).*

> *John Vervaekes "Relevance Realisation" we can now model as "care driven observations". We can say that what we see is that which is "relevant" as in what we "care" to observe to act the way we "care". And we can accept that our "caring" is self referential through our consciousness that enables us to experience reality from an "external" perspective and be able to feed back this aspect of reality into our "caring processor".*

> *From this model of the human actor in a human society we can conclude that Rational Reasoning is a support function to our caring process. The work in tandem. What we care about drives us to reason about specific parts of reality. And our rational reasoning conclusions are fed back to "program" what we care about in the future.*

> *We then can be tempted to observe that the thing we call "free will" as an aspect of our experience about how much relevant observation we are able to make and how many possible actions we are allowed to try out in any given context. If we feel we do not have access to the observations we "care to have" we feel unfree. Therefore we don't want people to lie to us. Nor do we like if we are not allowed to ask questions or go places to find things out. Further, if we feel we are not able to or not allowed to act in the ways we care to act we also feel unfree.*

> *But maybe it is time to bring back the realisation that the thing we search for is the self locking social system that "optimise" our experience of "getting what we care for". We come into existence to care for different aspects of a human life as a person and as a society. The things each and every one of us care for is not an indication of "truth". It is an indication of the need to keep our collective social system in a good position. The very reason we have conflicts is because a stable system needs counter forces to bring it together.*

> *We have evolved to care for our reality to act to fill the potential of a very constricted environment. We have not evolved to actually be free to get unlimited access to the things we care for. We have evolved to act in concert make "the best" of a harsh reality. To explore to find all the possibilities "our there".*

> *Absolute freedom should thus be regarded as our gate way drug to hell. It unleashes our different drives from balancing out and we "explode" as freed but lonely persons unable to cooperate for a "greater good". If we bring down "heaven" we also bring up "hell". It seems we must trust the collective "truth" over our individual one. The collective "care" must include but not be limited to what each member care for.*
