	==> It seems the ESC acts as a “Servo” controlled through the “Servo Wire”?
	==> My (and most) ESC provides the 5V power to the receiver controlled electronics tryout a built in BEC (Battery Eliminator Circuit) through the Servo Wire
	==> All servo wires are electrically “the same” plus-ground-signal but comes in two basic colour schemes (red-black-white and red-brown-orange) with minor variations.
	==> All servo wires signal wire uses PWM (Pulse Width Control)!

	==>

If I use my RC ESC I must implement the “standard RC servo PWM signal” (50 Hz 1-2 mS pulse width modulation) AND perform a startup-and-calibration-sequence. 

The ESC-to-transmitter calibration power-up sequence is stated in the manual as transmitter full throttle, ESC power on, transmitter zero-throttle (each step synchronised with acknowledge beeps from the ESC)

Each start-flying power-up sequence is stated in the manual as transmitter zero-throttle, ESC power on.

		==> It seems PPM is “simply” a fixed with pulse on the falling edge of the corresponding PWM signal (thus providing the same modulated time distance)?

			NOTE: PPM also serialise several channels into the same wire (a PPM pulse per channel)

			



	==> Consider this page about PPM vs SBUS servo wire protocol (https://oscarliang.com/ppm-sbus-d4r-ii-x4r-sb/ <https://oscarliang.com/ppm-sbus-d4r-ii-x4r-sb/>)?

		==> AHA! It seems SBUS and PPM are Reveiver-protocols (NOT servo wire protocols)?

“PPM and SBUS are both receiver protocols. PPM (aka CPPM) is an old, analogue signal. SBUS is a newer, digital signal."

	==> Consider this page that seems to state that all Services Wires uses PWM signalling (https://www.dronetrest.com/t/rc-radio-control-protocols-explained-pwm-ppm-pcm-sbus-ibus-dsmx-dsm2/1357 <https://www.dronetrest.com/t/rc-radio-control-protocols-explained-pwm-ppm-pcm-sbus-ibus-dsmx-dsm2/1357>)?

“PWM has been around for a long time in the radio control hobby and is the protocol that your ESC’s and servos talk to each other on. The analogue signal takes the form of a pulse, and the length of the pulse represents a specific value, with 1000 being minimum, and 2000 being the maximum value.

Each channel has its own single wire, so if you have an 8 channel receiver you will need to connect 8 wires to read the inputs into your flight controller."
