Could we draw some new conclusions by studying Steven Luke (Philosophy Bites interview ”Steven Lukes on Power” http://philosophybites.com/2015/06/steven-lukes-on-power.html) and his idea of ”Power” and relate that to the ”Power” of fictional believes?

1) May our ability to collaborate flexibly by believing fictional stories be defined in terms of ”Power” as defined by Steven Lukes?
2) Is such ”Powers” Collaborative benefits?
3) May we use such ”Power” concepts as isomorphic to ”We shall believe in that which makes us want to do what is beneficial to us”?
4) Can www argue that we shall promote ”Powers” that create emergences in the form of the most beneficial society?
5) Does Steven Luke take emergence into account when he evaluates ”power”? That is, does he regard a social organism as powerful if it is able to control emergence? A social organism (e.g. a group of privileged people) may benefit from create emergence of other social organisms (e.g. voters for a political party) that enables its privileges to continue?
6) May this concept of ”Power” even be the missing link to explain why act-in-faith may be powerful and thus beneficial even if the faith is in ungrounded and even false ideas?
