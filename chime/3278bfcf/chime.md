> "As if the gun is loaded”

> In the second Vancouver debate between Jordan B Peterson and Sam Harris moderated by Bret Weinstein they brought up the “As if the gun is loaded” rule of action.

> I think there is several deep truths to be extracted from this example about how we as humans has evolved two systems of augmentation of reality with truths. That emotions are truth about what we value and thus how we should act. And facts are truth about state and thus about what action is possible. And that we in fact much more reliably act in faith to values than we act in faith to state?

> Lets listen to Sam Harris explaining why he understands and accepts the importance of always acting “as if the gun is loaded”. And then I come back and tries to delve even deeper than Bret Weinstens concept of Metaphorical Truth.

> "As if the gun is loaded"

> Could the reason we should act in the presence of guns as if the gun is loaded - be that we can’t fully trust our rational representation of reality. That if we trust that we have really checked that the gun is now safe - that we can now represent the gun as being in a safe state - then we will eventually get the state of the gun wrong. And someone will get shot by mistake? So, the reason we should act as if the gun is loaded is something we have found out based on the mishaps that has occurred when we do not. And the only way to minimise such mishaps with guns, is to simply always assume they are loaded and should be treated as loaded?

> There seems to be an important and deep truth here about how reliable our human representation of the world actually is. Or rather, to what degree we can act in faith to our conscious representation of the world?

> Also, the “always act as if the gun is loaded” rule - holds within it a reference to how we should “feel" around guns. That we should work up very specific feelings when we are around guns. Feelings of respect and feelings of fear. Feelings of alertness and a feeling of urge to really pay attention to what happens with the gun.

> Could it be that the truth about how to act in the presence of guns is to NOT trust our conscious representation of the guns state. But instead try very hard to engage the correct emotions around guns. And trust these emotions to tell us how to act. And that

> acting in faith to these emotions  
> is better than acting in faith to our ability  
> to get the facts about the gun  
> correct?

> I think there is more to this story than just the discussion about metaphorical truth versus factual truth. There here seems to be a rational reason to pay attention to. lets call it - emotional truth. That the truth about how we should act has evolved and been encoded into our behaviour in the form of emotions. That our ability to act in faith to rational facts are in many cases of less reliability than our ability to act in faith to emotions.

> Now, I don’t think this means that our emotions are to be trusted more than rational facts. But it is a case for the fact that through evolution our ability to act in faith to emotions is much more reliable than our ability to act in faith to consciously derived facts.

> Basically - the “act as if the gun is loaded” is about values versus facts. Evolution has equipped us with a “truth" about how to act. And these truth are represented to us in the form of emotions. And emotions augments reality with values. Only later in our evolution have we added a layer of a conscious representation of the world. A representation that augments reality with facts.

> Thus we can argue that the our factual representation about the state of reality is an augmentation of the older and more powerful emotional augmentation about how we should interact with reality? The things we value tells us how we should act. While the facts about reality tells us what is possible?
