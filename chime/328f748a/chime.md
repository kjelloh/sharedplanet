There is something fishy with our concept of the subjective being of less "real-ness" as the objective .

> *So, what I want to try and bring to the surface is how we talk about Fre Will as being an Illusion based on our model of the physical reality as being deterministic. To me there seems to be a blind spot in our reasoning here that fails to adress the observation that the way Free will is an Illusion seems to loose all meaning of what we mean for something to be an Illusion. For example, if we say a mirage is an Illusion, we imply that we should NOT act as if the mirage tells us something about what is really there. or put another way, the mirage is an Illusion because if we act as if it is real we will probably die because when we arrive at the physical location where the mirage tells us there is water, there is in fact no water there.*

> *Now, when we say that Free Will is an Illusion it is unclear if we mean we should not act as if Free Will is "real". That is, if Free Will is an Illusion in the sense that if we act as if Free Will is real will will probably die. So as an Illusion it seems to be either a harmless one in that so far it has not killed us. Or, it is in fact a beneficial "Illusion" in that it seems to model something about reality that should guide our action?*

> *I have tried to listen to New Atheists, like Sam Harris or Daniel Dennett, to determine what they imply when they infer that Free Will is an Illusion. And so far it seems clear to me they at least mean that our model of Free Will does not fit our model of the physical world. Well, I would agree. They also seem to say that therefore we should NOT act as if the Free Will exist. Ok, this may or may not follow from the observation that the traits of what we call Free Will does not fit what can exist in a deterministic universe. Still, inferring what we should not do, does not provide an alternative telling us how we should interact with reality. What does it mean to NOT act as Free Will "exist"?*

> *Further, so far I have failed to hear anyone arguing for the non existence of Free Will, to adress the problem if it is even possible for a conscious human being to NOT act as if Free Will exist? Now this last question is crucial! We may argue until hell freezes over on what is rationally true, what we can infer about the physical nature of reality, and still be able to actually stop us from acting as if all our subjective experiences about reality is also trustworthy and "true"?*

> *So that is why I propose the analogy between the existence of "Free Will" being an Illusion of the same nature as "Beauty" being an Illusion. When we stand in awe before a stunningly beautiful sunset we will find it beautiful, self evidently and irresistibly, how ever much we may try to define the experience as subjective and thus NOT actually "real". Is it not so that the beauty of a sunset is an illusion is so irrelevant that in practice it does not matter? We actually don't care if it is an "Illusion" or not because it is simply self evidently REAL in a more importance sense to us. The beauty of the sunset is REAL in the sense that is relevant!*

> *Our Qualia seems to be the most real aspect of the reality we experience. And even if we "degrade" Free Will to a Qualia among all other like the colour "Green" and the "Beauty" of a sunset, it is still the relevant aspect of REALITY we find ourselves to live in? So much so that it is in fact the objective reality that takes second place? Or, if we look more closely, the objective reality just don't cover the aspects of what we experience as REAL and RELEVANT and thus falls short to help us navigate our lives into the future? In fact, we may go so far and say that the Objective, and inferred, reality, is more a definition of the restrictions of what is possible for us to do, rather than a model to help us decide what we should do, where we should go?*

> *With these observations and reasoning we find ourselves at the cross roads of David Humes Is-Ought dichotomy. It seems the Objective Reality is the "Is" part that defines what we can expect to be possible in our interaction with reality. While our Qualia-*  
> *embellished "Virtual Reality" we find ourself to experience (live in as most REAL and most RELEVANT) provides us with the "Ought" action options? And the reason there seem to be a dichotomy between what is and what ought to be done comes from us modelling reality as one that "exist" (the physical objective reality) and one embellished (only an "Illusion" in our minds) that therefore is "less true". If we instead model ourselves as living actors that has evolved to interact with reality using our subjective experience (our internal representation) of it. And respect that for some reason this "virtual reality" is the representation of reality evolution has selected for us as the "best fitted" or "the best one to to trust for acts of faith", then perhaps we can better reason about how we as a human society should understand reality?*

> *It is as if it is in fact NOT "Free Will" that is the Illusion we should identify, but our ability to interact with reality based on Objective Truth alone?*

> *But what then is the valuable and relevant nature of "Reasoning" and "Objective Truth"? Can it be that it is in fact an add-on to hone our subjective representation of reality? That we can use rational thinking and rigorous experiments and observation to stop us from acting in ways that physical reality will NOT let us do? That rational thinking is our tool to identify where our embellished reality is in fact fatally "wrong". Like, we trust we can fly but when we try we fall to the ground and die. That when new atheists argue the flaws of religions and superstitious believes they ARE in fact swinging Occam Razor to cut away at the parts of our subjective model of reality that is in fact less than correct?*

> *If so, if we bring back our conclusion that we still find our subjective reality as most REAL and RELEVANT, the place for rational thinking is to identify the restrictions of reality, NOT to define the map to navigate a human life? The core value of rational thinking is to reduce reality, clean up reality by mapping it to a formal system of only true-false dichotomies to provide Occams Razor with a target to cut away to reveal realities real restrictions. But it can never be the tool to draw the map for humanity to navigate a good life. For that we need to accept but maybe better understand the part of us we call the "subjective" that is our **unescapable well-being, ought-producing, good-life searching, unconscious engine**?*
