Both Larry Tempkin (in http://philosophybites.com/2015/07/larry-temkin-on-transitivity.html) and Bard and Södergård may be seen to have something to say about progress. Bard and Södergård argues that ”progress” is the idea to define the goal of modern society (progress in science, welfare, knowledge, self knowledge and career). Larry Tempkin discusses the problem of ordering by discussing that assumed transitivity (a relating to b reating to c means a relates to c) is not as true as we like to think. This would mean that progress may in fact be an illusion by reasoning that society in state a progressing to a ”better” state b and then to a yet better state c does not necessarily mean that state c is ”better" than a!

This would support the synteist opposition to put ”progress” as a goal of life and society.

For some reasons I like this approach and even more that Tempkin reasoning about broken transitivity gives strong support that it does not even exist!

This also rimes with the approach of a core-less ideology open to adopt what we reasonable know in aim for the ”best” living condition we can imagine.
