It seems to me that we can’t talk about integers having any meaning unless we also have something to count, i.e., categories?

1) Consider how integers are used to sub-divide a category into individs (and thus require a category, e.g., a Set of something, to exist)?

The idea here is that we take the existence of objects (things) so self-evident that we forget that these things exists only to us or organisms able to represent reality as one where these categories exists. It is so self evident that rocks exists. This one rock is a different individual rock separated from another one. But that all these rocks share the concept of being rocks and thus can be counted. But if we think about it we can argue that rocks exists to us only because we can experience rocks; We can manipulate rocks; We can interact differently with rocks then with e.g., flowers or sticks. But it is easy to argue that Rocks does not exist to bacteria. In the reality of bacteria they are unable to experience rocks because they simply lack both sensory inputs to populate their reality with representations of rocks. To bacteria Rocks also has no value. A bacteria can’t model the existence of rocks and somehow decide how to act based on the existence of rocks. So we have good reason to understand that a bacteria has no concept of rocks and thus no concept of counting rocks. But we can argue that Rocks also does not exist in the reality of flies or ants. To them the matter they interact with is the same whether it is a rock, the ground or the flat surface of a mountain. A Fly can’t move a rock and thus can’t experience that rocks are operate things from other solid things a fly may land on. A fly and an ant may be said to share a reality where no rocks are present.

So for me it seems we must first realise that objects are representation of our reality that we can act-in-faith to to be able to interact with reality in a recurse-able way. Meaning that if we act-in-faith to rocks being rocks, expecting our model of rocks to match our experience of rocks, then it also makes sense to add the concept of integers to our model. With integers we can represent aspects of the presence of rocks that could prove valuable ti update the model of reality of other human beings. We can speak “give me three rocks” and in that way communicate what service we would like others to do for us. In effect being able to cooperate and perform some algorithm where manipulation of rocks is a part.

So we, as being able to experience existence of objects, also can apply the meaning of integers as way of counting rocks. Where counting as one aspect is both a way to give names to rock individuals and at the same time group several rocks together into amounts of rocks.

But the whole time, I can’t see that then concept of integers can exist separated from the concept of categories. Someone or something must create a model of reality in which things exists before integers can exist. There are not “two moons” until some categoriser represents reality as containing "two moons”. From our own human alternative model of reality we know that there can at the sam time exist “two moons”, x number of iron oxide and y number of silicon atoms. These models does not contradicts each other. Tehy just provide models to act-in-faith to depending on what algorithm or taks we are carrying out in our interaction with reality.

2) Consider math is in fact a model of reality of different form of “counting” where the concept of the infinitesimal allows us to act-in-faith to reality being made up of continuous matter (as opposed to discrete objects)?

I wonder if it could be that whatever detailed model of reality we can come up with there will never actually be a model where anything continuous at the most detailed level is part of the model? What my intuition tells me is that our concept of the infinitesimal just is a way to enable math to behave the way we experience reality from our sensory inputs behave. We model water as a continuum with no limit to how small amounts of water we can interact with. But in fact, so far we have no reason to believe that water can exist as made up of infinitesimal parts of something out of which water emerges?

Actually, what I have learned so far all models of the smallest parts out of which reality is made of, comes in discrete quantities. In fact, the quantum theory is in some way this very idea. That energy, time and space are all quanta of discrete parts, objects, that is individual things ordredd into categories.

In this way calculus with infinitesimals are just a slick representation of reality that enables us to more concise model complex systems. We can model a pendulum using trigonometric functions although at the smallest level no cosine function can be used between the quantifiable locations of the pendulum.
