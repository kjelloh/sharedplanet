The first hour or so was about the news in TV OS and Watch OS and did not catch my attention or interest to any large degree. It was mostly about enhancement to media consumption and ego branding with emojis and things along those lines.

But the section about enhanced privacy was interesting and made me thinking two things at the same time. For one I liked that Apple has made it their business to sell privacy. The other was that as a user I still do not "own" my data. Meaning the data is still locked in into the apple eco system. So while it is good that Apple seems to charge a premium price for not looking at my data. They also keeps my data locked away inside iCloud and in proprietary format on my devices.

In a way Apple has also actually without anyone seeing it happen become the Big Brother they so fiercely fought in the famous commercial from 1984. Only with a twist. They observe us and has full control over the bullshit we get exposed to and have to believe to use their products just as Big Brother in the novel did. Only they do not "look" at the data for any individual surveillance. They do not need to. The role of Big Brother is not surveillance. Big Brother is just there to ensure we live the life we are supposed to and believe the reality that are communicated so that we remain in the community like good citizens. How empty or meaningless it at the core of things seems to be? The whole event was a perfect example of the Big Brother vibe that Apple increasingly is sending out. Every joke, every laugh, surprise, excitement and relaxed playfulness was carefully choreographed and rehearsed down to the millisecond. It was as real as amercian wrestling. And each time the presenter had to engage the audience with request to confirm how awesome the things they talked about where, each time the value at the core of what was presented deflated. There was actually very little to be excited about, just more of the same stuff we have seen for several years. So the introduction graphics with monkey and robot heads that exploded and emptied their bran content displayed as icons of stuff that we where not supposed to pay attention to, was there to remind us what to feel. In case we did not actually get our "minds blown" by the actual things we got served!

So the new Mac Pro heads down the hardware acceleration path?! That was kind of expected. But the way apple did it was to keep users hostages to use Apple hardware. No way to insert a standard graphics card as it seems. The Apple graphics card was impressive on paper. But it also was housed in a apple proprietary housing with both a PCIe connectors as well as an additional connector for Thunderbolt 3 and display port output? I suppose we will get more details from observers at the conference?

The presentation of the new display ended a bit sour and the audience even gasped! Apple had chosen to sell the display mount and the display stand separate from the display itself! I don't think anyone saw that coming! First apple indicated the display price to be around 3900 (I think it was) for the lower spec display and 4900 something for the higher spec:ed one. And that kind of hurt but was not any surprise as apple pricing goes. But to then top that off with stating that the stand costs 999 and the mount 199 something was surprising! It hurt and the audience gasped!

So the whole Mac Pro presentation ended quite abruptly and quite sour. I did not recover until several minutes into the Mac OS presentation, still lingering on what the Mac Pro and the new displays was all about.

The update to Swift was interesting. They improved the programming experience with a more graphical approach to defining user interfaces. All seemingly enabled by introducing a new Swift empowering framework called "SwiftUI" that seemed to replace the previous UIKit. But the approach as showed caed on stage was intriguing for me as a programmer. I recognised some of the concepts from Embarcadero VCL and Fire Monkey. And it also resembled to a large degree the run time coding ability of C++ in Juce. Now you get a simulated iOS device display in real time in the Xcode window. And you could change the code or the graphical layout and they both stayed in sync though the whole process. Potentially nice!

All and all I could not stop thinking that now more than ever do we need a way to live free digital lives. The Darwinet and shared Tha models would be very nice to have now. The Apple WWDC 2019 key note contained many services we as digital citizens would like to have. Like single anonymous (uncrackable) login and seamless sharing of data between devices and other digital emdividuals. If only we could have those services provided in a eco system not owned or controlled by the Apple company!
