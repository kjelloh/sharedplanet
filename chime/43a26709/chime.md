It seems we can extract interesting models of how our mind models reality from investigating the proposal that what we call insight, enlightenment, growing up, transcendence and so on are all examples of “growing” a meta-pattern through infernos or as emergence?

1) Consider what John Vervaeke has to say in “Ep. 11 - Awakening from the Meaning Crisis - Higher States of Consciousness, Part 1” about higher levels of consciousness (<https://youtu.be/39NpjQDtqNw?t=3326> )?

> “*…It’s not jus one error. It’s a complete systems of errors. And the way you go through a developmental change, what kids do is they find a systematic pattern of errors. They find an isight that is not just about one problem. But an insight that will apply systematically to that all of those interconnected interrelated errors. And when they have that systematically penetrative insight; When they found that nexus of errors so they can massively intervene on themselves, then they go through a developmental change and they grow up cognitively. They mature and that is what can be going on in the Enlightenment experience.*"

==&gt; It seems another way to model this experience is that we consciously experience a “higher” reality, a more “true” reality in the form of a meta-reality that adds clarity and correlations about a whole slew of patterns that was before separate “truths”?

2) Consider to what degree we can propose that “higher” insights are either inferred or does emerge?

==&gt; Consider that once we have internalised a set of patterns they are available to us for investigation and “pattern recognition” and so we may argue that maybe we are able to infer patterns through introspection?

==&gt; But also consider that by adding noise, practice things like shamanism, mindfulness or other disruptive practices (psychological technologies) we may engage our internalisation system to have higher level of patterns actually emerge (rather that be inferred)?

> *The idea here is that by “blurring” the patterns we study, by squinting at what we are looking at, by muffle the sound we hear, by forcing ourselves to imagine things are different than we believe etc, we can remove enough of the specifics to allow the higher common pattern of the “unspecific” to emerge?*

> *It seems we can argue that one of the mechanisms in play when emergent behaviour occurs is that sub-level cause-and-effect events cancel each other out in some aspects and attenuate one another in others. So that a higher form of behaviour manifests as a result of letting the pattern of several sub-pattern activities converge?*

> *We can think of the simple emergence of an interference tone from two tones of slightly different frequencies. The interference tone is a representation of the pattern of the two original tones being a specific difference in frequency apart?*

> *In a more complex example of an ant-society that collectively switches between food hunting and water hunting as an emergent behaviour from individual ants signalling lack of food and lack of water. Each ant act-in-faith to individual truth about the state of water or food deficiency, but the collective response tips over to the one or the other so that the ant-society as a whole seems to optimise between such choices.*

> *I have myself experienced that lowering or raising the volume of some music can enable me to better hear the lyrics sung. At least that was a method we practiced a lot when I played in a cover band and we tried to capture the lyrics of a song we wanted to play (this was of course before internet existed and the lyrics of a song was just a googling away…).*
