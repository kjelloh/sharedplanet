If, as one would think, the religion of the future requires interaction and communication, then what role may technology play in the religion of the future?

The impact of science and technology to society and our interaction may be discussed and analyzed from many point of views. For sure, one aspect is the enhanced ability it provides us with to interact and communicate. One direction of development seems to favor consumption of information, to most part perhaps for entertainment. One other is to provide individual channels to publish messages to friends and the public. We have seen the growth of social media where these two currents meet. Social media is used by the providers to broadcast commercial messages in the stream of messages from ”friends”. Anyone today may publish a web site, blog and post on Facebook, twitter, Instagram and youtube just to mention a few.

So, what can we say about interaction in the name of the religion of the future? What kind of information would we like to exchange? For what purpose do we exchange this information? What properties do we require from the information channels and the devices used for this communication?

1) The kind of information exchanged: This is not such an easy question as it seems. For one, we can not know of what ”kinds” of information there may now or in the future. We may define types of media (text, images, film, interactive games) or the type of message content (State purposes and goals, proposals for change, arguments for different actions, pure religious gospel).
2) The purpose of communication: Well the simplest answer would be to spread the gospel? But that would of course be a to shallow an answer that may not be complete, neither provide enough information to provide direction in finding answers.
3) Required properties of communication channels and devices. We should propose that the channel and devices used should not be affected by stake-holders with other incitements then those defined by the religion. This means that if the channel or the device has properties that are defined by e.g., commercial interests, then these must be taken under considerations to evaluate to what degree these properties may affect or even corrupt the religious purpose of the communication. Of course, we can’t ban commercially financed channels and devices from being used for religious messaging. This would most surely leave us with few or no tools to take advantage of the internet? At the very least it would disconnect the religion from taking advantage of the latest technological development. On the contrary, the religion of the future must, to be really influential to the living conditions of humanity, be a very real stake-holder in this very technological development.
