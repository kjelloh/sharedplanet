3) Consider what I can take away and possibly use from The Bunkie cabins design concepts in my own house build?

==&gt; It seems The Bunkie cabins are built from CAD designed CNC carved sheet wood parts (which I should be able to also use and possibly reducing the errors I make and be able to build the house alone)?

> *The idea here is that if I design the house to be assembled from CNC cut wood sheet materials I can rely on the model to ensure the house will actually go together as planned. I can also pre-think a lot more in the model phase and have the CNC to all the cutting and thus freeing me from having to re-understand every piece when I cut and build the house. With the parts marked it should be less stressful to build the house as the parts are already almost guaranteed to be of correct dimensions and all there?*

4) Consider what I need to do to ensure good water and wind proofing of the house if I build with constructed wood like sheet wood?

==&gt; It seems The Bunkie is designed for a dryer and warmer climate than the one we have in Sweden?

==&gt; The Bunkie cabins seem to all rely on a "tube" design where all teh structure are in the long side walls and roof. While the short sides are for windows and storage but add no actual strength to the design?

> *I like how The Bunkie cabins are close to the design I already have kind of fallen in love with. A clean, simple, rectangular box with smart inside decoration and storage. The house I liked in Sörfjärden and some container houses I have seen on Youtube all share this simple clean tube characteristic. With windows on both short ends it gets in the light I need.*

> *At least for my initial compact living quarters during my house build I should really think about putting something like The Bunkie together. And then perhaps gain more knowledge to decide on a larger more permanent house design?*
