I want to find a "better" way of reasoning based on observations that we need to go beyond trying to be right and instead trying to "care together"?

So the idea is that all reasoning is framed by how we care about reality and that we need to somehow model how our "caring" relates to "the society" that emerges from our act-of-faith to enable us to reason about it.

And one way to do this is to assume we are "self conscious ants" in that we interact as driven by what we "care" about and doing so become actors in a self organising system from which society emerges.

Thus we can propose that we should try to reason that we are in fact "emergence oriented programmers". We model reality to contain existence of socially constructed "objects" as if they statically "exists" when in fact they are emergent phenomena from our act-in-faith to the model of reality we silently and unconsciously develop while interacting with said experienced reality. And because we care about these social constructs we are in fact participating in reasoning about them to try and mould them according to our "values".

But then, if many things we care about are emergent self organising "objects", we in fact engaging in "emergence oriented programming" of the very social constructs we care about?

Can we come to trust a model of reality where we are conscious ants engaging in all our activities to have the social constructs we care about emerge? Can we in this way "lift" our reasoning from trying to make sense and be "right" about a deterministic correlation between facts and how we "should" act. Lift it to a meta-level where the correlation is in fact between acts-of-faith and self organising social constructs?

If so we can argue that "faith" is in fact vital as the "order of things" we really would like to se come into existence by "simply" act as-if it already exists! And this works because we can then self-organise in such a way that the thing we trust to be "true" actually then becomes "true"!

I think this is a valid presumption. So instead of arguing whether God "exists" or not we can elevate our reasoning to the question whether acting "as if" God exists or not will have society self organise into a "better" environment or not. We would then understand "God" as a believable model of "the unknown" that has built in rules about how to interact socially to have a "better" society (heaven) self organise into "existence".

We can do the same with the discussion between "The Left" and "The right" as manifest in Social Justice versus Liberal Conservatives where the question becomes if either of these models are "believable" by a majority and what society may emerge by act-in-faith to either of these models of reality?

In this way we can also argue that it is vital that we model the "unknown" to actually "care" about us. For one the "unknown" will then work as a tool for our reasoning in very much the same way as "eternity" and "infinitesimal" works as a tool in mathematics. By having a model that is capable of encompassing any reality we throw at it we are able to trust some ,ore or less common conclusion about how to interact with reality as a whole. And any model that limits itself to something "smaller" than the reality we experience we need to interact with, any such models will come into war with each other on who is "right" just from the simple fact they can be or even try very har dot be disjunct.

To enhance emergence of self-organising "best" society we should act-in-faith to a model that at least allows us to reason about how we "want" (care for) the unknown to be and thus have it become so!

From here we can ask the question if "the sacred ignorance" or "the sacred all knowing God" is the model to trust for act-of-faith to ensure we have the "optimal" society emerge (self organise)?
