One of our largest lies we tell ourselves is that we solve problem by rational thinking. Nothing could be further from the truth. We do not solve problems using rational thinking. The actual problem solving is made by processes totally unconscious to us. We like to think the solution to a problem comes to us by using rational thinking in a step-by-step process until problem is solved. In fact, it is more like as if we step-by-step is made aware of suggested solutions of how to move forward. We are consciously aware of these proposals as they pop up in our mind and we apply rational thinking to the proposal. Then we feed back that information back to our unconscious processes and a new proposal pops up. Now, If that is not how we consciously experience the process, it could be because our consciousness like to think it is in charge. Our consciousness are not aware of the huge contributions that are provided by our unconscious processes. But if we keep a focused attention of the "pop-up" aspect of proposed ideas we may be able to see the interaction actually going on between unconscious and conscious processing of the problem.

For example, I was reading a book when an idea pop into mind and I needed to put the book aside and write a note. Problem was I had nothing to bookmark the page in the book where I was reading. I did not want to put it down and spread it out at the current page. I wanted to put some peace of paper och some other object between the pages. My first idea was to use a printed receipt as a book mark as I have found these to work very well as bookmarks. Problem was I had no receipt. So the next idea to use a very specific receipt that had been laying on my kitchen table. Problem was I had thrown it away. Next idea was to find the receipt in the paper bin where I have thrown it. This idea made me go to the bin and look for it. And, behold, there it lay at the top of the bin. Problem was it was scrambled into a paper ball. This was easily solved by straighten it our and fold it to make it fit as a bookmark. So I did that and the problem was solved.

From this example I was able to examine how I experienced the problem solving process. I could have drawn the conclusion that I solved the problem by applying a series of steps of conscious rational thinking. But, as I closer analyzed this experience I came to the conclusion that this view could not be correct. There where serious gaps of unknown processing between each step of reasoning! First, when I needed the bookmark I realized I had none. But the idea to look for a receipt did not come from reasoning. I had used receipts before and thus should do so now. But the proposal to use a receipt actually popped up in my mind in response to the need of a bookmark. I did not consciously reason myself to do so. The idea ”came" to me. it was as if my consciousness was "made aware" of the proposal to use a receipt. I could then consciously determine that I needed a specific receipt. The process repeated itself when a new idea about using the receipt that was previously laying on my kitchen table popped up.The crucial thing to identify here is that I actually have no clue where that idea came from. That was for sure not an unconscious proposal! I was simply ”made aware” that that specific receipt could be the one to go for. It felt as if I consciously ”waved the wand” to have a problem solving proposal be revealed to me, and it was! This process now repeated itself over and over. And in each step I consciously "waved the wand” to get the next proposal created. And I ended up with peace of scrapped receipt neatly folded between the pages - problem solved.

Once one starts to see this conscious ”wand waving” going on and experience the problem solving proposals popping-up out of ”thin air” its hard to not be amazed. It is as if our consciousness is a conductor of some invisible orchestra and when we wave our conductor stick it plays notes back to us. But where is the orchestra? What instruments is there? And how does it know how to play the notes we like to hear? Well, thats a question to try and answer in another intellectual game. The crucial thing is to actually accept here that this is magic and unknown. It is a process that is not conscious and it is not rational!

By being aware of the interface between our conscious rational thinking and our unconscious and potentially irrational counterpart we may find ways of enhancing this process. Practices like talking to ourselves, scribbling on paper and reasoning with someone may in fact be to maximize the feed back loop to pur unconscious processes to get better proposals to rationally analyze. And the better we are at teaching our unconscious processing to provide god proposals, the better we may become to find the best solutions.

At some level we are aware of this unconscious part of ourselves. When it, on occasion, propose something really astoundingly new way of solving a problem we call it intuition. Maybe we should realize that intuition is actually going on all the time. It is just the degree of quality of what our intuition tells us that differs?
