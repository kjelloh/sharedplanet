In my quest to make sense of human action and reasoning I see a need to bring focus to our ability by acts-of-faith in accordance to a meaning-of-life narrative to have a "good" society emerge.

2) Consider if we view Religion as as a model of the whole human experience of living we can discuss both if the model is "true" (match reality) separate from if it is applicable (believable and trust-worthy)?

So, from Jordan B Peterson we get the observation that life is an exploratory "process" that through evolution enable us to internalise a model of reality that we trust to decide how to interact with it. We are driven by how we experience reality ought to be. And uses the observations we make about the discrepancy between how reality seems to be and how it ought to be to have us act and thus try to transform reality and our place in it to the more preferable state.

Now, if we add the observation that as humans we are very much like self aware ants. And, just as for ants, our society emerges from our collective interaction with each other and reality. That our society is in fact a self organising system driven by passion to transform it to what it ought to be. We can now describe this, or model this, as if we participate in the "programming" of the self organising society by executing a software that is our life governing narrative.

So we differ from ants only in that we have the ability to self reflect, to be self conscious, that we can apply abstract reasoning (computing with symbols in formal abstract systems that models reality) and that we can change our software (we can come to believe new things and re-frame reality using new narratives). But we are still actors in a self organising complex system we call society, culture and religion.

God in this model now becomes itself a model of something we believe to exist. Where this "something" is the all-encompassing "truth" or framing of our experienced reality. God can be seen as the model of whatever it is that we exist in both materialistically (physically) and subjectively (social and emotionally). And the question of "Is God Real" can be split into two questions. The first one is, "Is it something that God is the model of" and "Is God a model of that something"? In this way we acknowledge that our subjective experience or reality is what drives us and the part of reality we call objective is what restrict us. And "God" is a model of reality that enables us to reason about our subjective experience of living a human life in a human contexts.

Realising we are self-reflecting actors in a self-reflecting and self organising society enables us to understand that "God" may very well be our best model of everything that we experience and have to interact with. When religious people say "Live the word of God" it is a way to use language to represent how we can apply this model to guide our own actions to have society emerge for either change or for stability. The model of reality encapsulated by the model "God" is the result of evolution through time where humans has tried to represent reality in the forms of language and actions. Where a crucial part of the reality to explain is the social reality humans experience to participate in. And that is why religion has stories and rules that governs all forms of human interactions with the focus on how things "ought to be". The religious system of practices, truths and model or reality in the form of "God" has proven to have a society self-organise and self-stabilise as a self organising complex system of interacting self-aware actors.

It is worth mentioning here that what the materialistic model of reality as currently provided by physicalism in the form of a computable formal system expressed in the formal language of mathematics and logic so far mostly deal with direct cause-and-effect aspects of reality. And although very powerful it fails to enable us to reason with any potential correlation between our subjectively driven actions and the emergent social behaviour that comes out of these actions. The famous Hume observation that you can not get an Ought from an Is focus on this very aspect of reality. But as living organisms we do nothing but create Oughts for Is-es! That is the whole purpose of being alive. To interact with reality so that our place in it and the nature of it becomes one where we can repeat our own ought-producing model or reality.

The very thing we talk about using the word "ought" is the truth produced by our internalised model of reality that drives us to productively reproduce. But not only that. The things we are compelled to "ought" also have us investigate and transform our model of reality. When we act-in-faith we are in fact trusting our internalised model of reality as it has evolved through time, as it has been taught to us through our culture governing narrative and how we have experienced and reasoned ourselves into believing to be true. The religious narratives and trust in God now makes sense as a model of this whole shabang of a process. We act-in-faith to our ought-producing model of reality. We observe as in experience the outcome of our actions. We access the discrepancy between the transformed reality and the reality how it ought to be and we adjust our model. We than trust our new model by new acts-of-faith. And this process transforms us as actors in reality to equip ourselves with some more or less consensus about what model or reality is the "true" one. Being True in this sense meaning our actions line up with the reality that actually emerges from our actions in the self organising system we call society.

3) Consider we can understand collectively emergent behaviour, as in ant and human societies, as a computable program where the execution transforms the program itself?

So this idea is still slippery to me. But my intuition seems to pick up an aspect of self organising complex systems that they seem to be executing a program that at the same time is part of reality and also transforms this reality. As if the self-organisation comes from the the fact that the program that is executed it the same as the reality the program transforms. As if a computer program when executed affects only something external to the program itself while a self organising system when executed affects itself.

We can build some intuition about this by looking at three bodies in empty space. It is a known mathematical system in physics where we know it is not analytically computable in that we can write a formal equation that spits out the position given the coordinates and properties of the tree bodies. We have to iterate through time and step by step calculate how the three body interacts to optionally find out if their position converge to same stable configuration. We know our model according to Newton of the how the three bodies interact through the attraction of gravity correctly model how the behave in reality. But when we compute these outcomes we discover that each time we calculate a new position of one of the bodies we also change the reality of the other two and thus there psotion have to be re-calculated accordingly. Only to discover this process repeats for each body position we compute. So what we have to do is iterate until we discover if the three bodies settles into stable positions or if the drift apart or goes into an eternal stable revolution around each other. The intuition here is that the three bodies form a very simple and objectively computable self-organising system. 

Now if we jump to the ant society we can observe that the emergent behaviour form ant interactions it shares this three body interaction trait. The action of each ant affects the reality to be interacted with by the other ants. So the very reality of ant interaction each ant has to deal with changes with each action it takes itself. But from what I have understood from research on ant societies the rules that guides each ant, although simple, still changes while the drives for the three physical bodies to interact stays the same. So ant interaction adds complexity to the system behaviour by having ant action be driven by rules being activated or deactivated depending on the ant-to-ant interaction signalling. Where the ant signalling by exchanging chemicals antenna-to-antenna carries a representation of the ant society reality. Combined with each ants own ability to experience reality thought its own senses its ability to act is much wider that the actions taken by "dead" physical bodies under gravitational force interactions.

Now let's look at human interactions. We are also actors in a social reality that is itself affected by our own actions. But we are self aware. So our actions are affected by truth about our reality that includes ourselves and others. And not only that. We are able to invision how our actions may affect the future. In fact, we base our actions not only on the state of the present. We also simulate the future so that we perform acts-of-faith to what our model of reality tells us future will hold for us. So again, as humans we carry around "software" what can evolve with the reality we create. And this software is able to "compute" with our own drives and subjective experiences. And I would propose this software also produces our experiences. So we can learn, or discover, things we experienced with pain we now experience with indifference or acceptance or even Joy. So when compared with ants we add even more complexity to the ability of our interactions to self organise into ver complex realities for us to live in.
