Because I am an idiot?

Or am I calling out that the emperor has no clothes?

Here’s the thing - I want a desktop. And I want to continue to run my OS X applications.

What are my options for the coming say five years?

1) Well, I can continue to use my Mid 2014 Macbook Pro 15” Retina laptop. It served me well while I was working as an IT consultant where I needed a very portable and capable computer for software development and document writing.

But now I spend most of my time working here at my desk. And the MacBook Pro is does not seem to be designed to operate with the lid closed?

And why should it - It is a laptop for crying out loud!

2) I can get me one of Apples newer “desktop” computers.

I can choose between an iMac, an Imac Pro, a Mac Pro or the new Mac Mini.

It takes not more than a quick glance to see that Apple is really not focused on providing desktop computers! Not to mention how they fail to provide modular ones?

Firstly, the iMac and the iMac pro are fully integrated screen and computer solutions. So for me to go down that route would turn my current display, keyboard and mouse superfluous.

I need a new computing unit. I do NOT need new user interaction devices!

This leaves me with a choice between the 2013 Mac Pro, the “trash can” or the new Mac Mini.

Now, the trash can is five years old. And the Mac Mini is not designed for graphic computation!

Ok Apple - Thus sucks! You surely are treating the tradition and values of Steve Jobs rather poorly?

Or, to be fare - Steve Jobs also steered quite heavily into the post-computer realm. So maybe I should not be surprised?

Anyhow - I am currently not mobile. And I want to have good, reliably computing power right here on my desktop.

I simply want good desktop computer power that just works. And that does not struggle when delivering that power!

The last point is really quite crucial to me!

==&gt; I want my computer to do heavy lifting without a fan ramping up in pain!!

From the information I have managed to gather the 2013 Mac Pro is known for its very quiet fan - Even when it ramps up. And it keeps the CPU from heating above 80 to 90 degrees C.

While the iMac, the iMac Pro and the Mac mini sets the uppe bar to around 100 degrees for the CPU! Running the fan with an annoying hight rate and noice.

First - I observe that the CPU has not gotten any faster since 2013. In fact - the only thing that has happen is that the overclock capacity (what commercially has been named “Boost”) has gone up. Apparently by allowing the CPU to run at 10 to 15 degrees celsius higher temperature (the iMac Pro CPU temperature peaks at continuous 100 degrees C while the Mac Pro 2013 peaks at 85 to 90 degrees).

The Mac Pro comes with Xeon CPUs that runs between six core 3.5 GHx (Boost to 3.9) up to 12 core 2.7 GHz (Boost to 3.5 GHz). (Xeon E5-1650v2, Xeon E5-1680v2, Xeon E5-2697v2)

The iMac Pro that comes with Xeon W CPUs with eight core 3.2 GHz (Boost to 4.2 GHz) up to 18 core 2.3 GHz (Boost to 4.3 GHZ) (Apple proprietary Xeon W-2140B (downclocked Xeon W-2145), Apple proprietary Xeon W-2150B (likely a downclocked Xeon W-2155), Xeon W-2170B (probably related to Xeon W-2175), Xeon W-2191B (likely related to Xeon W-2195) )

The new Mac Mini comes with CPUs from four core i3 3.6 GHz (No Boost) to (I3-8100B, I5-8500B, I7-8700B)
