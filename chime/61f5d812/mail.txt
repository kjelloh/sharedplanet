Some of the possible base materials of hot-melt adhesives include the following:

Ethylene-vinyl acetate <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethylene-vinyl_acetate> (EVA) copolymers, low-performance, the low-cost and most common material for the glue sticks (e.g., the light amber colored Thermogrip GS51, GS52, and GS53)

Polyolefins <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyolefin> (PO) (polyethylene <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyethylene> (usually LDPE <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LDPE> but also HDPE <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HDPE>; HDPE has higher melting point and better temperature resistance), atactic <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atactic> polypropylene <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polypropylene> (PP or APP), polybutene-1 <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polybutene-1>, oxidized polyethylene <https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Oxidized_polyethylene&action=edit&redlink=1>, etc.), low-performance, for difficult-to-bond plastics.

Polyamides and polyesters, high-performance

Polyamides <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyamide> (PA), high-performance, for severe environments; high-temperature glues; typically applied at over 200 °C, but can degrade and char during processing.

Polyesters <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyester>, similar to the ones used for synthetic fibers <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_fiber>. High application temperature.

Polyurethanes

Thermoplastic polyurethane <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyurethane> (TPU) offer good adhesion to different surfaces due to presence of polar <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_polarity> groups. Their low glass transition temperature provides flexibility at low temperatures. 

Polyurethanes (PUR), or reactive urethanes, for high temperatures and high flexibility. New type of hot melt thermosetting <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermosetting> adhesives, introduced in early 1990s.

Styrene <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styrene> block copolymers <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_copolymer> (SBC), also called styrene copolymer adhesives and rubber-based adhesives, have good low-temperature flexibility, high elongation, and high heat resistance.

Polycaprolactone <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycaprolactone> with soy protein <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy_protein>, using coconut oil <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_oil> as plasticizer, a biodegradable <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodegradable> hot-melt adhesive investigated at Korea University <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_University>

Tackifiers frequently present most of both weight percentage and cost of the hot-melt adhesive.
