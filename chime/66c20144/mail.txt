The main idea here is to take the strandbeest walking leg linkage design and use it for the bicycle front suspension design?

I imagine mounting the wheel axes through the "foot" of the linkage mechanism and have the driver rotation be replaced with a spring.

We can accomplish this by mounting two strandbeest "legs" on each side of the front wheel and attach then to the crown and steering tube.


Copyright https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Strandbeest_Leg_Proportions.svg/300px-Strandbeest_Leg_Proportions.svg.png <https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Strandbeest_Leg_Proportions.svg/300px-Strandbeest_Leg_Proportions.svg.png> 

From the example above (from wikipedia article https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/OpenSCAD_User_Manual/Example/Strandbeest <https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/OpenSCAD_User_Manual/Example/Strandbeest> ) it seems if we use the bottom part of the red path as the travel of the front wheel axel up-and-down we need the whole mechanism to be roughly twice the width of the travel?

So a travel of 120mm (red bottom part of path) would mean a mechanism that is 240mm in diameter (but slightly oval i shape).

One revolution of the green path results in one revolution of the red path. So give-or-take we need a spring to be able to travel around half the revolution of the green path.

Note though that the ration between the travel of the driving green path and the red "foot" travel is not linear. I still think though we can expect that half a springed revolution of the green path will suffice?

FINALLY - I imagine we can make this mechanism as a compliant mechanism? We then get a springed linear motion along the bottom red path without any moving parts?!

1) In the naive approach we "simply" makes the joints compliant and place a torsion spring at the drive (green path in figure)?
2) If we think about it, maybe some of the linkage arms in the design can be bending to somehow enhance the design and simplify the mechanism (get rid of a spring at the drive location)?
