We like to think we build our understanding of things from a core and then expand knowledge to a bigger and bigger body of truth.

But given the strong evidence (arguments and observations) that our knowledge and understanding is both groundless (no firm base or core) and limited (the universe will always contain more information that our minds or computers may) this image of truth seeking may be wrong. A better one would be to realize that the core does not exist. We always start of with some premises that defines the boundary of what we ”know” to an empty (unknown) core and then expand both inwards (what do we know that is the most reliable foundation or knowledge) and outwards (what can we say about reality) from there.

A better image of this state of affairs is that we build a circle with a hole in the center. The inner perimeter is our defined perimeter to the unknown (groundless) core. The outer perimeter is our frontier to better knowledge about reality as a whole.

If we imagine our knowledge body as a three dimensional ”donut” we may even say that our perimeter to the unknown core and the unknown reality as a whole is in the same space of unknown (or yet to know). The unknown core and the unknown reality is part of the same unknown space of yet-to-be-known.

1) Note that this is very much the symbol for Synteism ( ![](Synteism_logo.tiff)) although the center emptiness here actually touches the outer emptiness. One would also argue that the knowledge body (the black) in this image is divided into two halvs, although implicitly combined by the boundary (infinitely thin) outer circle creating the whole. Somehow, the donut metaphor seems a better one for our knowledge body?
