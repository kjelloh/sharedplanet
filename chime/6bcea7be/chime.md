It seems we can learn from comparing what we take for granted of physical identity of objects that are not so true when compared to digital counterparts?

1) A physical object is unique in the spec time (there are no two identical objects)

2) A digital object may exists in several identical copies (every bit is identical)

3) Physical objects can’t be copied but digital ones can.

==&gt; So far there are no “beam me up scotty” that re-cerates a copy at a new location (disintegrating the source)  
==&gt; But for digital objects this is commonplace (e.g. by streaming digital data over a network)

==&gt; Such an object would only be allowed to re-locate using a special “mover” (that re-encrypts the content to reflect the new location)?
