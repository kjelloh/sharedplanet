Would it be a good idea to have a car crumple-zone based on converting the impact energy (deceleration energy) to heat by having water converted to steam?

1) Wikipedia seem to discuss crumple-zone designs here (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crumple_zone)?
2) Wikipedia seem to discuss static impact attenuates for road block applications, some based on water, here (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_attenuator)?
3) Water is a medium with a high  enthalpy of vaporization and thus would allow a small mass to transform large energies into heat?
4) Now, heat is damaging to humans so maybe this idea is not a good one (may cause har to nearby humans)?
5) What does a rough calculation of the amount of water needed to put a car to a halt?

	==> Car mass m_c = 2000 Kg
	==> Car Speed  v= 150 Km/h = 150 000 m/ 3600 s = 1500/36 = close to 50 m/s
	==> Car velocity energy Ev = 1/2*m_c*v*v = 1/2 * 2000 * 50 * 50 = 2.5 MJ (mega joule)
	==> Energy to vaporize m_w mass of water Ew = { to vaporize water requires 2260 kJ/kg} = m_w * 2.26 MJ / kg
	==> Thus if we use the whole car kinetic energy of 2.5 MJ to vaporise water we can vaporise 2.5 / 2.26 = 1.1 kg of water?!!

	==> But then we have not taken into account the energy required to first heat the water to the boiling temperature, i.e., 462 J/(kg K).
	==> So vaporising m_w mass of water from 20 to 100 degrees (80 K raise) and then also vaporise it would require m_w * ((462 J/(kg K) * 80 ) + 2.26 MJ / kg))...

Ok, so the energy required to heat the water is almost redundant in comparison to turn the water into steam so we can disregard that term it seems.

	==> On the other hand, to ensure NO water is vaporised we need enough water so that it is only heated and NOT turned into steam (safety reasons)?

Let say we want the water to not be heated more than 20 degrees - say from 20 to 40 degrees celsius. Then m_w * 462 J/(kg K) * 20 <= 2.5 MJ. This would require m_w <= 2.5 MJ / 9.24 kJ = 270 kg. So this checks out. If we use water filled absorbers on roads that are filled with more than 270 kg (i.e. litres) they can absorb a crashing car to halt into pure heat. ANd as the water would probably also  gain some kinetic energy we are "safe" from secondary harm from hot water being splashed around?
