1) Consider the wikipedia article on Occam´s Razor ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occam%27s_razor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occam's_razor) )?

> “*...the problem-solving principle that essentially states that "simpler solutions are more likely to be correct than complex ones." When presented with competing hypotheses to solve a problem, one should select the solution with the fewest assumptions.*”

> “*...the preference for simplicity in the scientific method is based on the falsifiability criterion. For each accepted explanation of a phenomenon, there may be an extremely large, perhaps even incomprehensible, number of possible and more complex alternatives. Since one can always burden failing explanations with ad hoc hypotheses to prevent them from being falsified, simpler theories are preferable to more complex ones because they are more testable.*"

==&gt; So it seems we can say that Occam´s razor is a principle where we only expand the complexity of an explanation when a shorter explanation is not (yet) available?

==&gt; Also, there will always be a much greater set of more complex equally “good” explanations to any given short explanation (so always choosing the shortest one at least aims at not build larger models of reality than we really need to)?

2) Consider what objections we can provide to criticise the principle of “Occam´s Razor”?

==&gt; Consider applying Occam´s razor may lead us into a “local minimum” of explanation complexity that is NOT the global minimum?

> *We could argue that depending on what explanation we start with we could, after pruning the explanation from excessive complexity, end up with a small model of reality that is NOT the optimal smallest one for the optimal largest set of behaviours of reality we have observed? So there should be some rational reason to actually try and keep a more complex explanation in so far as this enables us to “prune” it from different kinds of complexity that leads us to a lower final complexity model?*

> *Now, as we do not know what parts to “prune off” in a more complex explanation this may prove to be ineffective, or even impossible, to apply in real scientific research? If we try to keep explanations that explains more than we have observed without being required to explain that which is observed, or that provide more than one explanations of the observed, we risk to fail in the endeavour to determine the most exact and single model that we expect reality to behave like?*

2) Consider what it would mean to “Shave God with Occams Razor”?

==&gt; Consider the idea that God is a model of how possible ways we “should” behave to optimise a recurse-able social system emerge?

> *The idea here is that the most complex reality we as humans interact with AND are dependent on is the intelligent behaviour of our society? If we also focus on collective social behaviour that actually emerge rather that is directly caused, we can at least argue that the model of such a reality and how to interact and navigate it is a very hard problem in deed?*

> *Question now then becomes if “God” as an external all-powerful “Will” can be seen as a model of the for us unknown correlations between my own actions, my fellow being actions and the actual social reality I find myself in?*

> *For one we can argue that from Darwinian Evolution we can suspect that we evolve not only to adapt but also to internalise a recurse-able model of the very reality I as human is also a co-creator of? So the model of this social reality that emerge from human collective interactions based on individual act-in-faiths to our observations of how such models correlate to the observed reality could very well be an external all-being “being”, a deity, which we may ask questions and carry out preferred actions?*

> *In this perspective, how can we prune such model of reality expressed as a story about “God”? Does it follow from the flaws of “Gods Words” in writings we have uncovered from our successful model of the natural sciences that the whole “God” idea is a totally failed model for the stuff we still is unable to provide alternative answers to?*

> *For example, Lets say “God” explains to us, in writing and in practice, what the highest meaning of a human life is? And at the same time natural sciences have discovered that much of the old writings and practices according to said “God” has been found inferior to such found by science. Here we are talking about how to cure deceases, how to tame energies to create comfortable lives and how to rid human lives of all kinds of material related suffering. Very few would question that these scientific discoveries has not proven to be “better” than corresponding truths provided in writing and practices of say the christian religion? Still, if we are to apply Occam´s razor to the model of reality to enable us to interact and navigate reality in a collective way to optimise how we relate to all kind of human pain - then it is not so clear that the whole “God” concept gets cut off?*

> *What natural sciences can say is that the model of “God” or the “Wording of Gods message” is off in the light of our better model of the natural sciences. But this could just as easily mean thet the model of “God” has to be updated, NOT that the whole model is wrong to apply?*

> *In natural sciences the things we have most successfully explained using very small models represented in mathematical and social language, are those with a very direct cause-and-effect relation between interaction and outcome. We know how much harder it is to build models of systems that exposes emergent behaviours. Also, in natural science so far we have focused on systems that are made up of physical objects that interact in space and time. We have NOT gotten very far yet to model the reality we as conscious being find ourselves where we Care about things.*

> *If we propose that as humans we live in a reality where we consciously Care about how things should be; Where we Care about how things are; Where we Care about what other humans Care about. Then we need a model that are able to model at least this “Caring” aspect. From this proposal follows that we can NOT reduce the model of human reality to one where we only deal with space-time object interactions and predictions. Now, looking at the model we represent as the story about “God” - is there any part of this model we would like to keep, or represent in a modified way - to incorporate the models we have made of space-time reality?*

> *So far I find it actually irrational to dismiss “God” on the grounds that some (or much of) wording of the “God” model is contradicted by the natural sciences. I am still an Atheist - but it is more a statement about me accepting that the wording of the model of “God” needs tweaking - rather than a statement that a model with explanations like those historically expressed as “Writings of God” is no longer required.*

> ***We need a model of how to interact with, negotiate and navigate the human reality of collective emergent manifestations. A reality where the most prominent and yet unexplained existence of “caring about the state of things” is a central part. This model requires a wording (a representation in language, symbols and practice) of such nature that, as close to as is possible, every human can internalise and apply it to their lives. So far we have observed only one language and representation that is “digestible" applicable by almost all humans - The telling of stories!***

==&gt; Consider “If you bring heaven to earth you also bring hell” if true, tells us we need a model of reality that contains this explanation in an applicable way?

> *The idea here is to try and understand if we risk something we can call “Hell” to emerge in the human reality where our inner-world of “caring” exists if we try to actually force something we could call “Paradise” to manifest in our lives and society? Or put another way - if we try to maximise the existence of the things we care to exist also means that the things we care to NOT exist will almost causally emerge as a result?*

> *For one, we can begin with an observation that our emotions may be viewed as a “truth” we experience about how to act (or not act). And that these emotions evolved in an environment where we as humans only could only balance the pain against the pleasure in a very limited way. It seems we can argue that our emotions tries to have us act in a way to optimise our lives within the constraints of what nature allowed us to accomplish?*

> *Now in the comfort and safety of modern life and societies we no longer are required to compromise. We can choose to pursue a life where we feel only positive emotions with the aim to also never feel pain. But this reality may actually not be the one our emotions had evolved to help us navigate? We could argue that in the past scarcity we benefitted from pursuing sweets to eat because sweets where so scarce. But today we not only have sweets available everywhere, we are even constantly persuaded to buy and consume it! We can make the same observations for every emotion that rewards us with pleasure when we accomplish what it urges us to pursue. We enjoy high energy foods but gets fat if nothing restricts us from over-eating. We enjoy to indulge ourselves with entertainment but get lonely and alienated if all we do is binge-watch Netflix on our free time. We enjoy sex but fails to stay in stable family constellations if we don’t settle down with one partner for at least the time of bringing up children. We enjoy to relax but find it dauntingly boring if we don’t have something to relax from. The list goes on.*

> ***So at least from the observation that an excess in activities that are strongly correlated with positive emotions we seem to also automatically engage in activities that is bad for our health and wellbeing. So, in a sense, if we take a short-cut to “paradise” we can’t help but also bring “Hell” in the form of dysfunctional body functions and social interactions?***
