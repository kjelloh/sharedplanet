1) Consider in what way we seem to be heading back to a hunter gatherer society?

The observation we focus on here is the one that every business that do work for money do better if they can do the work without human labour. Why? Because automated work has no limits to how cheap it can be, how fast it can work and for how long it can work. Secondly humanity invented tools and technology to empower it with super powers to transform reality to their likings. We fought off dangerous animals, we tamed the fire, we produced clothes and we discovered mathematics to model reality that enabled us to harness nearly unlimited amounts of energy to produce both material and thinking tools to provide for our needs.

Money is energy to make humans work. One human will work for money because she can then trust she can use this earned money to have another human work in turn. It is a self actualisation truth about value. I value money because you value money. I work for money because you work for money. It's a genius invention of act-in-faith to a truth that is true as long as everyone acts as if it is true. The value of money emerges from human behaviour acting in faith to the very existence of the emergence itself.

But the act-in-faith to money is not a recurse-sable truth. Each time we work for money the reality about the value of money is changed. The perceived value of you work changes the reality in which your work is applied. Your work transforms reality including the value of your work, and thus also the value of the money you earned for your work. When we say that the act-in-faith to the true value of money is not recurse-able we mean that each time you actualise this act-in-faith the thing you trust gets transformed. And the transformations of the value of money that your work produces does not converge. It does not stabilise in some equilibrium. We know this because we know the economy must not stagnate. No, the economy must grow. In fact growth is the true indicator of a healthy economy. An economy that will be able to provide wealth for humans who work. And the reason for this is that so much of the work humans do are of the nature that it must pay off in the future.

Thew act-in-faith to a growing economy is the second indication we are heading towards a hunter gatherer society. In a growing economy humans will take loans to invest today in the hope that what they invested in can be sold at a higher net profit tomorrow. The invested money is used to pay humans to work and everyone wins. Only that part of this equation is that it puts tremendous pressure to also reduce the price of production. Which has proven an easy thing to do since the industrial revolution accelerated the increased efficiency in production. Each human and machine could be made to produce more in the same amount of time. The reduced cost to produce together with competition lowered the price of the products but this was securely compensated for by the increased production. Combined with the raised wages that enables workers to also purchase more the economic boom was a fact.

2) Consider what may replace money when it is no longer required nor applicable as to make human do any work?

In a society where all production is fully automated, including energy harvesting and self repair and maintenance, there will be no need to pay any human to do any work. In fact, the whole reason money was such a genius invention in the first place, was the very fact it worked as energy to feed a human to get some work done. The very fact humans would work for money was precisely because humans would do work for money. Money gained value by being a self-referential value. I want to work to get money because then I could pay another human to do some other work.

But if no humans are required to work, then money no longer has a value. Now we may argue that there are still things a human would like only another human to do for them. But then, would those inter-human actions be of such nature that money exchange is possible? The very nature of having another human do something for money is in itself a sign of an effort the other human would not do by her own accord. So at least we can suspect that the only paid work that a human would require from another are such where the very act of paying is part of the service. And such services would hardly be any foundation for any stable economy. Think about it - For a human to provide s ervice for money requires that the receiver could use the money to purchase some service in her turn. And we have already reasoned us into concluding that only services of the kind that forces a human to do something she would otherwise not do, is the only purchasable services available, given that everything else is available for free by the automated production machine.
