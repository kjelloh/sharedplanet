I keep poking at my intuition that there is something fishy with rational reasoning that does seems to leave out another way living things discovers and explores reality and in this case how faith can be a clue into something that complements rational reasoning?

So the idea here is that if we observe that our ability to reason about reality is a very resent addition to our capabilities as living actors navigating the obstacles and possibilities of life. What if we try the proposition that rational reasoning is our ability to compute with an abstract model of realities causal relations. Then perhaps the value of faith is to drive us to traverse the unknown? The things we talk about as faith seems to be strongly correlated with being able to do irrational things. And while this in the light of the enlightenment has been looked upon with frown eyebrows I can't escape all the observations that we still value faith driven actions, that we emotionally rewarded for cultivating our faiths and thus that evolution and iteration over time seems to suggest that faith is valuable for our ability to overcome and explore reality?

So faith may modelled as composed of at least two mechanisms. One being our ability to trust a vision about a better future. Something that we evaluate as being better that current reality. The second being our drive to transform reality into becoming that which the vision tells us to strive for. So faith is both a truth-producing and action enforcing "motor" for us to transform reality in situations where we are unable to compute a path of transformations to some better state.

Consider Sam Harris "Moral Landscape" as his vision of a model of reality where we can always compute reality transformations that objectively or subjectively leads to a better reality. In his vision he has eliminated the need for faith based actions. And we can raise the question if this may not be an error of judgement in failing to observe the value of faith based actions? If for example Sam Harris Moral Landscape is not made up by a surface where the slopes defined by transitive better and worse relations. If this is the case we can argue that you need faith to navigate and explore this landscape. With rational thinking or computation with cause-and-effect abstractions is reduced to restrictions, not motivations. An intransitive better-worse shaped Moral Landscape means you can move uphill to iteratively better and better realities and still end up at a worse place than you started. So to explore and find the truly "better" places to be you need a vision of this better place and act-in-faith driven navigation of the landscape to get there?
