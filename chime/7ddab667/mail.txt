Advantages[edit <https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Vertical_axis_wind_turbine&action=edit&section=2>]
VAWTs offer a number of advantages over traditional horizontal-axis wind turbines <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal-axis_wind_turbine> (HAWTs):
Being omni-directional, some forms do not need to track the wind. This means they don't require a complex mechanism and motors to yaw the rotor and pitch the blades.[8] <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_axis_wind_turbine#cite_note-8>
VAWTs generally function better than HAWTs in turbulent and gusty winds. HAWTs cannot efficiently harvest such winds, which also cause accelerated fatigue.
the gearbox of a VAWT takes much less fatigue than that of a HAWT.[citation needed <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed>]
In VAWTs, gearbox replacement and maintenance are simpler and more efficient, as the gearbox is accessible at ground level, so that that no cranes or other large equipment are needed on-site. This reduces costs and impact on the environment. Motor and gearbox failures generally are significant considerations in the operation and maintenance of HAWTs both on and offshore.
some designs of VAWTs in suitable situations can use screw pile <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screw_pile> foundations, which hugely reduces the road transport of concrete and the carbon cost of installation. Screw piles can be fully recycled at the end of their life.
wings of the Darrieus type have a constant chord and so are easier to manufacture than the blades of a HAWT, which have a much more complex shape and structure.
VAWTs can be grouped more closely in wind farms, increasing the generated power per unit of land area.
VAWTs can be installed on HAWT wind farm below the existing HAWTs; this can supplement the power output of the existing farm.[9] <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_axis_wind_turbine#cite_note-9>
research at Caltech <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology> has also shown that a carefully designed wind farm using VAWTs can have an output power ten times that of a HAWT wind farm of the same size.[10] <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_axis_wind_turbine#cite_note-10>

Disadvantages[edit <https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Vertical_axis_wind_turbine&action=edit&section=3>]
One of the major outstanding challenges facing vertical axis wind turbine technology is dynamic stall of the blades as the angle of attack varies rapidly.[11] <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_axis_wind_turbine#cite_note-11>[12] <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_axis_wind_turbine#cite_note-12>[13] <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_axis_wind_turbine#cite_note-13>
The blades of a VAWT are fatigue-prone due to the wide variation in applied forces during each rotation. This can be overcome by the use of modern composite materials and improvements in design - including the use of aerodynamic wing tips that cause the spreader wing connections to have a static load. The vertically oriented blades can twist and bend during each turn, causing them to break apart.
VAWTs have proven less reliable than HAWTs <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine#Horizontal_axis>,[14] <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_axis_wind_turbine#cite_note-14> although modern designs of VAWTs have overcome many of the issues associated with early designs.[15] <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_axis_wind_turbine#cite_note-15>[16] <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_axis_wind_turbine#cite_note-16>
