Could ot be a more fruitful model of human lives to compare it to a “Random Walk” (strongly shaped by “Her Randomness”) than a deliberately planned Cause-and-Effect chain of events?

1) Question - How correlated are events in a human life (place of birth, choice of education, first job, first love, friends, impact of nature events, job proposals)?

2) Question - How much do we react rather than act on events that occur (loss aversion, aim at the same life as all others, buys the latest phone, wear the same fashion)?

3) Question - How aware are we to emergent phenomena (family, neighbourhood, local and country society, global economy)?

4) Question - To what degree can free will impact life events (System 1 dominates System 2, System 2 Free Will may only veto System 1 proposals)?

…

Consider, in the light of the strong indications that free will impact is very limited (even if it exists) and that where we are, how we relate to life and what events actually occur in our lives are in fact totally out of our control, that we should embrace a model of human lives as being a “Random Walk” and thus design practices to relate beneficial to this fact?

a) Consider our Actions as the equivalence of throwing a dice or attend a lottery? To enable change we need to attend often to “games” where we seem to be able to “win” the change we strive for?  
b) Consider also our limitation (or inability) to reason what the emergent effects may become? A large group may act-in-faith to get “good” result but create bad emergence. And vice versa a group where all act-in-faith with smilingly “bad” result may create good emergence?  
==&gt; Consider the behaviour of System 1 to “deep learn” patterns are in fact evolutions answer to cope with unpredictable emergence?  
c) What practices in a religion-of-the-future may we design and try to cope with life being a “Random Walk”?  
==&gt; The practices should confirm and adress existence of “Her Randomness” and “Your Emergence”?  
==&gt; The practices should aid to act-in-faith in attending the beneficial “games” and accept weak correlation between action and outcome?  
d) Consider the Economical impact that those who are rich has been “lucky” rather than having accomplished it?  
==&gt; What better forms of “return of investment” for individuals and communities can we device that beter serves the “common good” but still carry enough incentives to take dividual risk?  
e) Consider practices that help us “cope” with loss aversion when actions seem to lead to bad result (but that still is better than the bad results of being passive although the latter feels less painful)?  
==&gt; As Kahneman found in “Thinking fast and slow” we are emotionally biased to “not rock the boat” also when in fact it is bad for us?
