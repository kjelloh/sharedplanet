> *I can't help but getting a stronger and stronger hunch that our blind spot is how mysterious our ability to observe actually is. That we seem to be able to use the input from our senses, seemingly operating on something that is computation, and from that deduce what "is" out there. Not only are we able to map sensory input to an internal representation of what "is". But we also seem to add as secret ingredient of "discerning" or "understanding". As if we know "more" than just pattern match sensed reality to our model of this reality?*

> *Now I know this is a weak argument. But Penrose seem to share my hunch that we are missing something when we observe ourselves and our ability to compute; to reason. We have succeeded to simulate our ability to perform computation, or reasoning. But we have yet to simulate the ability we have to "understand", to get what stuff "means". As Gödel himself observed - we seem to be able to build models of computation from rules or facts that we identify as axioms. These axioms are not provable within the system. Gödel showed the computation we can use to prove that systems we trust must use axioms. If not the system becomes inconsistent and thus untrustworthy. The point that Gödel observed is how we are able to reason with things that are self evidently true, although they are computationally unprovable. This IS mysterious and we must take this seriously. It just does not seem honest for us to rely on the faith that now when we are on the way to master computation we are also on the way to get to "observation" as in "understanding".*

> *Now I still think we, and thus our brains, exist in the same reality as the things we model with the natural laws of physics. So if our brain is able to perform operations that are not computational in nature, then this ability must be something allowed by some model of reality he have yet to find and represent.*

> *It is intriguing that "observation" is also built in so strongly, and carry the same mystery of what is its nature, as the tailing part of the Schrödinger equation in quantum physics. As Penrose also notes the Schrödinger equation is a computable component that produces a result that seems to correlate well with a probability distribution of the outcome of some initial conditions. But then the theory requires a separate "observation" of the system for it to decide which one of the different possible outcomes it will settle in. Something it will refuse to do until being exposed to this act of being "observed". At least we can try to explore this connection in such a way to actually define "observation" as closely correlated to this crucial event in the quantum physics model of reality. Could there be a connection to our ability to perform this operation of "observing". And thus also tie the ability to "observe" to our ability to "self-observe" i.e., to experience ourselves as being conscious, of having a consciousness?*

> *Further, we could try to throw in "free will" into this pot of mysterious things we experience to exist or at least what our own model of reality has as a crucial part. Let me first just say that for me the question if our will is free or not is actually not what I find most interesting with "free will". What I find intriguing is that in our model of our own experienced reality we value our ability to execute an operation called "free will" and we hold each other responsible to do so with great care. As if doing something by "free will" or "coercion" are two very different things. Where coerced bad behaviour is excusable but freely chosen bad behaviour is something to punish. If the universe is computational in nature this makes no sense. And evolution should not have selected for humans who acted out a value to make this distinction from those who did not? I can't help but see a connection here to our ability to "observe" separate from being able to "Reason". As if free will somehow is more about taking care to "observe" while reasoning is an add-on to extend meaning beyond the self-evident, the "observed"? As if reasoning is our ability to compute with our "observations" that we have added on to our older and more evolved ability to just "observe" and act emotionally?*
