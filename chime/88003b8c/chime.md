> *So, can we dig out some interesting observation here? Molyneux proposition is "All organisms require universally preferred behaviour to live". Now this sentence uses a term "universally preferred behaviour" without defining it. Or rather, it defines "universally preferred behaviour" as that which organism require to live. So we can make the argument that what Molyneux is trying to do is to refer to a model of reality he has internalised and that he represents with this term. Now this is very much what in logic is called "begging the question". The sentence is supposed to state an isolated fact. And then this fact together with some other facts should lead to the conclusion that there is such a thing as "universally preferred behaviour". But that is not what Molyneux is doing. He starts of with referring to "universally preferred behaviour" as something that already exists in his own internalised model of reality.*

> *Now, our hypothesis here is that we humans can never truly ground any facts about reality. We can only accept there is a limit and plant an axiom there. Or, which is the main goal of our discussion here, we weave the truth into themselves into a truth-raft. A truth raft where one truth follows from another but none gets ever grounded.*

Something is true because if we act as if it is true, the outcome of the action complies with the truth. Or as an aforism "it is true because it is true". As a whole it "begs the question". The reason we don't see this is because in rigorous models of reality or of logic or mathematics we hide the "ungroundliness" of the model as a whole behind axioms. In "thin air" we plant what we want to be anchors to truth about reality. When in fact we only locate the truths we observe but cant sem to ground in anything but that they just self evidently are true. Oen plus One is two. Why? It just makes sense! It just works!
