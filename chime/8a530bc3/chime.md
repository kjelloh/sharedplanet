> *So the basic idea here is that numbers are always "about" something. And what they are about is always a quantity of something of "the same kind".*

> *So numbers always exists in a representation of a model of something where the model contains "sets" in some sense. Or put another way, if every "thing" in the model is unique, then sets of things of the same kind would not "exist" as each object and each set of this object would be the "same thing".*

> *So numbers are an abstraction of the quantity of "things" of the "same" kind. And "same kind" is a meta-model of the model in which the individual "things" exist. Finally a meta-model is a model about the model so we can express the conclusion that for numbers to "exist" as a representation of something it must be of things in a meta-model and thus the whole representation must contain self references.*

> *To be more concrete we can say that the number 4 (four) is a representation of the quantity of "things" that remains "the same" however we permutate the "things". We can reverse it and say that if the quantity change if we permutate the "things" then there are NOT four of that thing.*

> *In this sense the number for exist in a representation of the physical world for "things" with the "same" quantity. We can imagine early humans discover the concepts of numbers when they gathered food for their fellow tribe members. They discovered that if they collected one fruit for each tribe member and brought it home, each tribe member would get a fruit regardless of how the fruit was distributed. There where four tribe members if four fruits could be distributed so each member got one fruit and no fruit was left over.*

> *Maybe in some early days humans collected fruit by keeping record of what member of the tribe got what fruit collected. So he or she collected one fruit for Adam, one for lLila, one for Knot and one for Ewa. And then gave Adam his fruit, Laila hers, Knot his and Ewa hers. And maybe they though no more about this for generations? Or maybe they quickly discovered that they could "count" the fruits by assigning a name to each fruit but then know they did not actually have to remember which fruit was Bens, which was Lailas and so on. As long as the "count" assigned a name to each collected fruit they could trust each member of the tribe would get a fruit.*

> *Just for fun, maybe the first numbers actually was the name of the members in the first tribe that discovered that "counting" was enough. They could treat "fruit" as a set of same "things" and the tribe as a set of the same "things" and as long as the "count" was the same they could pair up things one-to-one?*

> *But the bottom line is, numbers exist in our representation of reality where we are able to also represent meta-things like sets with quantity of thing of "same" kind.*
