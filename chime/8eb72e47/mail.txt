We seem to be wired to search the truth. We are curious beings, in company with a lot of fellow animals. We have developed the concepts of mathematics and logics to deeper probe the truth of reality ungraspable by experience alone.

But we are also quote happy to accept ungrounded truths and also kling vigorously to truths that others just as vigorously claim to be false. So what can we tell about the benefits of aiming at getting ”reality" as correct as possible in opposition to simply go for the most beneficial ”wishful thinking” instead?

1) Evolution has equipped us with senses that provides us with a suitable user interface to the world but one that hides reality from us (we get a desktop with icons hiding the complexity of the computer all together). Evolution tells us a gentle lie is better for us than the cold truth.
2) On the other hand we are also equipped with the ability for scientific, mathematical and logical thinking. And so far it seems they actually helps us close up to reality as-it-is rather than reality-as-it-seems.
3) Then again, we see what we want to see and tend to rationalize why reality is what we expect rather than to shape our beliefs to what reality really is. We over value information that confirms our beliefs and tend to diminish or totally disregard information that contradicts what we believe. This may tell us we prefer truths that are beneficial to us rather than the truth that best fits what reality is telling us.

So, what are the arguments we should favor reality-as-it-is? Especially when it comes to the beliefs we adopt as borders to our unknowns.

a) Why should we face the irreparable flaws of our existence (our groundless knowledge, our mortality, our insatiability)? In what way will our current and future life be better if we do that? Why not sooth ourselves with the ”lie” that everything can be all right if we just do the right thing?
b) Why should we try to understand reality-as-it-is instead of just bee satisfied with reality-as-it-serves-our-needs? Now the simple answer may be that we can’t stop searching. We are driven by a never satiable curiosity to keep asking questions to understand the world and ourselves.
