At the heart of monetary capitalism lies the concept of ownership, the right to own and the protection of ownership by society rules and institutions. But humans is known to have lived and still in small numbers live in social contexts where ownership is not adopted. And what about other animals, do the incorporate the concept of ownership into their way of living? In the human society we can see both benefits and downsides of applying the right to own when it comes to share the resources of this planet. so what is the verdict? Should we continue to apply the idea of ownership or can we imagine another relation to resources that are more beneficial to humanity and our lives on this planet?

1) What does evolution tell us? Does creatures that adopt ownership ”win” over creators that does not? Animals that protect a territory or a flock to promote mating is s form of ownership on nature. The male takes ownership of a piece of land to attract females to mate with. Or protects a herd of females form other males to be abler o mate with them. Some birds and rodents collect food and hides it for scarce times. This is a type of ownership. They hide the food to protect their ownership over it. They have earned the food and thus is entitled to it over time. But humans is the only species that extended ownership to cover in principal all resources and even invented the idea of money as a neutral value of capital to value all own able stuff on the same scale of value.
2) Cold a better system be to look at our planet and all resources as owns by humanity as commons? Each individual gets a planet share byt birth right and thus gets a fairs share to participate in a free market to crate society emergence?
3) Why should humanity be the sole owner of all resources of this planet? If resources can be owned, should not all life have the right to compete for ownership? Perhaps humans, able to realize this and realize humanities dependence on what other living creatures provide, should appoint agents that act as capital holders form animals and plants? This would then be to take the idea of ownership to its extreme. How would that work and would the free market with such actors enable emergence of a system that is most beneficial to our needs? Will the emergent system maximize our deep richness?
4) Should we abandon the idea of ownership in favor of protection of other rights? From the point of Deep Richness, are there other values that matters more than resource ownership. No-one owns the air we breathe but everyone owns the right to polite it? No-one owns international waters byt everyone owns the right to travel it or fish in it?
5) Should we on the either extreme extend the idea of ownership to other aspects of life, like the right to breath, seek information and express our ideas? Should we all own such rights and others like them, e.g. the right to freedom and the right to not having to obey laws we have not approved? And in that case, would ownership of such rights also be valued and be transacted as a form of capital? Is this the way to enable humans to be able to earn deep wealth?
6) What can we say about the relationship between ownership and earning? To what degree, if any, should ones right to own something depend on how and if it was earned. Earning is closely related to fairness and a form of equality and justice. We may accept someone to own more if we feel they have fairly earned it. But what does this mean? Is current model of rights to own and way of earn the most beneficial for deep richness? What can be an alternative model to earn deep richness.
