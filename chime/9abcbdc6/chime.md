Donald Hoffman in interview answers that we currently know of no specific benefits of consciousness that the brain can’t provide without it.

Is this true? Is there nothing beneficial about conscious reflecting on our own behavior? Could it be the very aspect of consciousness creating the feeling disconnected from our body and brain that carries some benefits?

It feels like being conscious about our own or others behavior carries some benefits we can’t obtain unconsciously. What can those benefits be? It seems the most striking aspect of being conscious is our ability to experience existence within our body that seems to be, in some sense, separate from the body. Yes, we are aware of the seniority input form our body. Yes, we can speak with an inner voice and listen to it. We can experience inner imaging of places and events. And we can experience and reflect on feelings. But all this seem to occur outside our consciousness, as if we use our consciousness to watch them occur.

So, can we reason our way to a suggestion what benefits this experienced separation may provide for us. What can we do better with this separate consciousness that we can’t accomplish through unconscious brain functions?

1) Could it be benefits from our ability to process information from another point of view, although a virtual one? In the same way as an extra eye that we could send out to watch whats behind that bush over there would give as a beneficial new point of view of the room, the consciousness can provide us with the benefits of being able to reflect on reality from an extra reasoning point of view? Still, it is hard to find an example that we can apply this reasoning to to make it verifiable.  
2) Could it be wee need consciousness to be able to communicate efficiently with others. Maybe we need to be conscious to emulate what is going on in other conscious minds and thus be able to efficiently understand and communicate with them? Now other animals is able to exchange signals in some form of language, they are even able to express and communicator fears. So basically this is not unique for conscious humans.  
3) Could it be we need to be conscious to be able to imagine what another person knows of feels about me or a third person? To imagine what others think of a third person or me is a form of extreme separation from my own brain process and may thus benefit from conscious processing? Not only can we experience ourselves from the inside, we are then also able to better experience others from their inside? Being aware of the separation of others from ourselves also makes us aware of our own separation from ourselves?  
4) Could it be that to take the best advantage of the very complex user interface our brain creates for us we need to be able experience a separation from our body and mind? The conscious mind makes the best use of the user interface? If we where not conscious we would not question the user interface in the same way as we can being able to reflect on our own inputs and behavior? This lines up in some way with other findings that suggest we make all decisions unconsciously but are able to consciously veto them. Maybe our vetoing algorithm is most beneficial to us when applied consciously?

5) What if the purpose of consciousness is to drive us to seek meaning? If we where not conscious, we would not need meaning. We would just need to act and react. But being conscious also means wondering why and what the purpose of me, life and the world is. Maybe evolution would favor meaning seeking creatures? Maybe evolution favors religious creatures?! Also, feeling hope requires a consciousness? All other feelings work unconsciously? You are afraid, you flee or defend yourself. You get angry, you fight. You fall in love, you mate. All creatures share some degree of these feelings and does not require a conscious to benefit from them. But hope, that is not possible unless you are conscious about yourself. Could it be that simple. The extra strength of faith and hope is the edge we get over unconscious creatures? The strength of faith and hope enables us to put all our other abilities together to excel where we would otherwise fail. And evolution rewarded us with a conscious for that very purpose?!

Update: Hoffman states in an interview (at 4:20 in http://www.closertotruth.com/series/why-did-consciousness-emerge#video-2412) that his position is that what is behind the user interface is a consciousness. That consciousness is the fundamental thing that exists and that spacee and time and objects are just in our user interface!

Now, does this kill the path about the benefit of consciousness being the ability for hope based on religious beliefs? Maybe not? From his point of view evolution applies only to the objects we experience in our user interface. Consciousness itself provides the framework for, and thus is not subject to, evolution. Strong stuff!
