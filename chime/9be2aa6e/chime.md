In a ”banned” TED talk (http://www.sheldrake.org/tedx-whitechapel-the-banned-talk and https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1TerTgDEgUE) Rupert Sheldrake lists 10 science dogmas that he argues hinders full science understanding of reality. He also proposes a theory of his own called "morphic resonance" that could better cope with both changing laws of physics, evolution and how our minds actually work.

The TED blog posts (http://blog.ted.com/the-debate-about-rupert-sheldrakes-talk/) about the controversy and publishes the talk there for registered users to see and watch based on the argument that the claims of Rupert Sheldrake may not be fair scientific assumptions and perhaps should be viewed as missinformation.

1) Never the less, the core of his questioning the constant of physical laws is actually shared by Smolin and perhaps others. Although Smolin, not others may agree on the theory of morphic resonance, he may agree on the assumption that the framework of reality actually changes with time.  
2) His claims also kind of resonates with the approach taken by Donald Hoffman about consciousness being behind all and our time-space actually are framed within a system of consciousness.  
3) It is also interesting to note ones own reaction when listening to this talk. When he touches on psychic phenomena like telepathy at least I automatically reacts with distrust and fear. Bu then I also think that, to be scientifically open minded, means I should not feel threatened but just rely on science to digest such claims and see what comes out.

http://www.bokus.com/bok/9781594773174/morphic-resonance/ and http://www.bokus.com/bok/9781444727944/the-science-delusion/ are two of his books.

He is on Facebook: https://www.facebook.com/RupertSheldrake

At the very least, this talk may serve as a study in how instinctively we refrain from ideas and claims that does not rime with the ones we already adopted.
