1) Consider what I can learn from performing the NCS tutorial on their home site (<https://education.ncscolour.com> )?

> *Basically it seems the system is based on perceived colour values (not colour substance mix rations or physical light wavelength energies or similar).*

> *Further, the system is based on four base colours Yellow, Red, Blue and Green chosen from human tests to find the "most" agreed upon "pure" colour of each kind. So the Yellow selected is the one that most humans finds the purest yellow with no Red, Blur or Green in it. And in the same way the Red is the statistically "purest" perceived Red, The Blue is the statistical "purest" perceived Blue and finally the Green is the statistically "purest" perceived Green.*

> *Then, a "Hue" is defined perceived pairwise mixing of the pure colours on a circle where Yellow is at the top, Red to the right, Blue at the bottom and Green to the left. From Yellow to Red the circle is divided into 10 equidistance steps, each defining the colour perceived as having 0...100% Pure "Redness". Again, these colours do NOT define how to actually create the colours by mixing Yellow and Red. They only define how must Redness the Yellow colour is statistically perceived to have.*

> ![](Colour-Circle_SMALL-1024x327.jpg)  
> *The NCS Colour Circle defining the perceived mix ratio of each colour pair in each quadrant*

> *The NCS Hue notation then, as an example, define a colour perceived as having Yellow with 60% "Redness" as Y60R (Yellow perceived as having 60% redness).*

> *On top of the Hue the NCS system now defines the "Blackness" and "Chromaticness" combined to define a "Nuance" of each colour on the colour circle.*

> ![](Colour-Triangle_SMALL-1024x327.jpg)  
> *The NCS "Colour Triangle" defines how the perceived "nuance" of colours are perceived to have a percentage of "Blackness" combined with a percentage of "Chromaticness"*

> *Now note that 70% blackness does NOT also mean 30% whiteness! No, the system is defined so that "Blackness" + "Chromaticness" + "Whiteness" is 100%. At first this seems not intuitive at all. But I suppose you can think of every colour to have its own "whiteness". So that if you perceive a colour to be one of the colours on the circle with some "blackness" in them, you will simultaneously perceive the whiteness as the opposite of "blackness". *

> *Anyhow, the NCS coding of the "nuance" is notated as perceived "percentage blackness followed by Chromaticness", e.g., 6090 means perecived as having 60% blackness and 90% "Colour" as in "Chromaticness".*

> *But I actually like this system. It makes sense to me to look at a colour and imagine how "Reddish Yellow", "Blueish Red", "Greenish Blue" and "Yellowish Green" it is. And then adjust the perceived colour to be a percentage of "blackness" and a percentage of the actual colour, i.e., "Chromaticness".*

> *Now I imagine it will take years of opractice to actually imagine the colour from reading the NCS code. But I have already found that when trying to match an existing colour to an NCS colour on a sample strip I can "see" if the sample I am holding up is to the correct one on the circle and if I have matched the correct nuance. So reaching for the next NCS colour pad to try against the target colour feels quite intuitive.*

> *For example, I tried to find the closest NCS colour that matched my apartment entry door. And by comparing I could estimate if the sample I was holding up against the door was too "greenish" or not enough "reddish". And then also see if the sample was to pale or too dark. So I can work with this system.*
