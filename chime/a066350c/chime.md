1) Consider that any actor that implements a processing of reality that takes into account its own wellbeing will evidently develop a self-conscious and a will to act?

> *The idea here is if we imagine an actor that has the ability to act to promote an imagined state of reality in which its own wellbeing is preserved or enhanced must, almost by definition, internalise something we may call self consciousness and a will. The observation is that for wellbeing to have any meaning the actor must model itself in some coherent way. And this model is then an awareness of itself as separated from other actors or physical reality. From that follows that to be able to act to transform reality into a state where the actor itself experience a higher state of wellbeing, it must be able to imagine and also predict what such a future state should be. And this imagined future state is what-ought-to-be.*

> *Now, if the preferred future state, the what-ought-to-be, is itself an emergence from collective actions of others like itself, then for being able to "program" what ought to emerge seems to require that each actor is self-aware and is driven by a will? Because only then will each actor be able to model its own actions in relation to the collective actions and adapt to affect the emergent collective action behaviour?*
