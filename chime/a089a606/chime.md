I stumbled on this talk on the youtube channel for Viscon 2016 and found it to be quite interesting in how it suggests one can think about communicating as a channel of Attention limited to the band width of for Exposition of my idea into the head of somebody else I am communicating with?

1) Thi seems to be the home on youtube of the talk ”Animation, Pacing, and Exposition — Tony Chu” (<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z4tB6qyxHJA&list=PLlgxAbM67lYKz_NssZ1DdDwIRIWbkDlN-&index=10>)?

2) At 1:40 he seems to make the point that if the Goal is Exposition then the constraint is Attention and the Tactic (method?) is Animation & Pacing

==&gt; At 2:20 - Exposition seems to mean reconstruct somewhere else - as in forming a concept in my head into the head of someone else (exposition of the concept)?

==&gt; The constraint is the attention of the receiver (no attention means no channel to transfer ”data”)

==&gt; Attention has two sides, Vision and Shert-term-Memory

==&gt; Vision is about what the brain passes through and filters out (and pleasing design helps the vision see the things we want to transfer)

==&gt; Short-term-memory limits the pace we can transfer information (human short term memory is only a couple of bits and when crammed things will fall out of attention)

==&gt; Cool model of an arcade car game as race against time - As designers we need to extend attention before it rans out just as the car in the game needs to reach the next check point to get more run time.

==&gt; Another model is faucet and sink - as designer we need to keep the sink of attention filled by draining attention as slow as possible and filling from the faucet qitck enough

==&gt; And the tactics (methods or tools) to do this is Animation and Pacing (adjust the transfer rate for the receiver to be able to process and consume)

3) At 6:20 he lists what Gains and Drains Attention

==&gt; Surprise, Delight, Order and Coherence Gain Attention

==&gt; Time, Decision, Clutter and Confusion Drain Attention.

4) He talks about us western reading order to place information in Value Proposition, Intrigue, Action order

==&gt; In the west we read from left to right and from top to bottom.

==&gt; Thus we know ho to place things so the we read the Value Proposition first, then the Intriguing items and end up at the Take Action item (e.g. Click to scroll for more and deeper info)

==&gt; Note that ”Click" is a decision (more energy) than ”Scroll” which is a Continuation (less energy to just continue what is already going on = go with the flow)

5) At 20:00 he talks about how we need to put all dependancies into the receivers head to communicate an idea that depends on other ideas.

==&gt; Ideas depend on other ideas just as software depend on other software.

==&gt; We need to break down ideas into its dependent part to make them come across (not be too big to get stuck in the attention channel)

==&gt; NOTE: This is a misstake I often do. I go for communicating the top idea - leaving out all the base of that idea and the receiver is left confused.

6) At 22:30 he stresses the point that Order Matters!

==&gt; We can’t communicate an Idea before the information it depends on is communicated (it must already be in the short term memory of the receiver)

7) Note that he seems to suggest to go bottom up (but then also top down to show where the sub-information fits into the big picture)?

==&gt; Like in his Lego instruction example where the instruction shows step by step very detailed how to build the item.

==&gt; But then the instruction also shows how the built sub-items fits into the final item.

==&gt; So - It should not be bad to present the final idea first but then take real care of breaking it down to communicate why the final Idea may be correct?
