Consider we can make the percolation process more efficient in terms of paint we can extract and re-use and how much fresh water we need to add to each cleaning process?

Start off with say four (4) empty containers for extracted paint. 
Fill container 2,3 and 4 with some amount of fresh water. 
Percolate water from container 2 trough the PET with roller into container 1.
Then percolate water from container 3 through the PET with roller into now empty container 2.
End with percolating water from container 4 trough the PET with roller into now empty container 3.
DONE?

The idea here is that in each step we use water with less paint than in the roller to percolate out paint from the roller. In the process getting a paint solution with increasingly high amount of paint. That is, the leftmost container will have the highest paint concentration.

Now assume the leftmost container potentially has a paint concentration that we actually can re-use? Then we can empty it back into the container of paint? Lest also assume we can store the content in container 2 and 3 by putting a lid on them and re-use them the next time we like to clean a roller? This means any succeeding percolation cleaning process will start of with some paint solution in container 2 and 3.

Start off with container 2 and 3 having the saved paint solution from previous percolation process.
Fill container 4 with some fresh water.
Percolate solution in container 2 trough the PET with roller into container 1.
Then percolate solution in container 3 through the PET with roller into now empty container 2.
End with percolating fresh water from container 4 trough the PET with roller into now empty container 3.
DONE?

Sounds simple enough? Could this be worth trying out?
