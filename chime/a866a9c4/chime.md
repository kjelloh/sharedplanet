Having spent over a year investigating new technologies and trends by reading articles and watching videos on the web I have one thing to say - please learn to ”cut to the meat”. Staring off with talking about the background, the problem to be solved, providing sales pitch or just diving right into all the details on how to install and configure the ”stuff” is NOT cutting to the meat.

1) Consider to come up with metaphors that shows how wrong it is to put download, build, install and configure at the intro of a tutorial?

==&gt; Consider a tutorial of how a car works that starts of with travel directions to a garage and a shopping list for tools?

==&gt; When describing how a car works you don’t start with setting upp an environment to work with a car?

==&gt; Download, build, install, configure software is just to get the ”world” of the software program come into place (it is not required to teach how the software is used)

...

2) Consider for someone browsing the web the first thing you want to know is - how do I use this?

==&gt; Consider **when we are scouting for information we want to find, we do not want to think!**

==&gt; Consider when you click a link you do it because you want to answer the question ”what is this?”.

==&gt; And the best answer is to show what it is (not show how to get it to work or talk about how great it is)?

==&gt; Consider the visitor want navigate to where it ”feels” right, not to where it is rationally right to go (they do not want to think to find)?

…

3) Consider ways to effectively cut to the meat when greeting someone curious to know what you provide?

==&gt; Consider to provide simple examples that reveals the ”pattern” of what you are providing?

==&gt; Consider to trust humans are ”pattern recognizers” and wants input for this process?

==&gt; The pattern they want to learn is the one answering the question ”does this interest me?”

==&gt; Thus, consider to provide information that engages with the curiosity of the visitor?

==&gt; If the visitor asks ”What can I use this for?” - then provide patterns that shows what happens when you use it (as a side effect also shows how it is used)?

==&gt; If the visitor asks ”Does this provide for my needs?” - Then provide several patterns the visitor may quickly look at to conveniently recognize if there are ones that provides for the needs?

…

4) Consider to organize your information as a maze with patterns as cross-referencing signs?

==&gt; Consider to divide your information into parts that resonates with a small set of patterns of the visitor are looking for?

==&gt; Then make these parts cross-refernce using links in the form of these patterns?

==&gt; Consider the visitor does not actually ”read” but do more trigger on the ”impression” (the patterns) the references gives of an all truth levels of the visitor (space-time, emotional and rational)?

==&gt; Consider the visitor is more likely to be guided by ”gut feeling” (pattern recognition represented as emotions) than by reasoning (rational thinking)?

==&gt; Rational thinking is more energy consuming and thus takes more effort to engage?

==&gt; And when we are required to engage rational thinking we get irritated (it feels like work and **when we are scouting for information we want to find, we do not want to think**)?

...

5) Consider to organize the tutorial into meta-layers of information (i.e., information, about the information and about the about the information)?

==&gt; The Download, build, install and configuration is meta-information about how to be able to bring the software into life?

==&gt; Consider motivation for the software functionality is meta-data about what the software can do?

...

6) Consider to provide truth on all the three human meta-truth levels experienced world, emotional meta-world and rational meta-meta world?

==&gt; One can for example acknowledge an observed phenomena and attach emotions to this state of thing?

==&gt; Then one can argue rationally about this to provide the information in the best possible (three channel) way?
