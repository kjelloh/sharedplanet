In "Om språkets evolution”, "När började människan prata? Och varför pratar vi?”, fredag 14 augusti kl 20.03 (http://t.sr.se/1DQDEZb) they talk about the evolution of the human languages. They ask the question why we have evolved the very complicated ability to produce consonants and vocals in very rapid flow of long complicated series?

Could it be that it started of in the same way as for birds. We competed in ”singing” these complicated sounds to create sexual attraction. The ability to ”over produce” in an activity that has nothing with survival to do chows that we are very fit and may carry exciting traits that may benefit offsprings. This borders on the whole scope of our cultural abilities that, to some part, has been explained as this kind of over ability in traits not needed for basic survival.

If so, then humans may have evolved this complicated singing or sounding before we started to talk with words. And then at some point we realized that we could pick part of this singing in consonants, vocals and voicing to carry specific information. Thus, instead of plainly sounding or signing to refer to a lion one could start using ”l i o n” as the specific pattern of sounds that means lion.

Maybe this thought is part of the bag of theories already? But at least I find this idea interesting and find pleasure in thinking about it this way :)
