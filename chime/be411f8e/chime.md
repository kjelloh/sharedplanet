> *So the idea here is to take something like "Conway's Game of Life" and have the produced pattern of cells behave as if they follow a simple mathematical "law" of cause and effect. Now we know this program at its core is deterministic in each step but we want to know if the rules of each cell development can produce some other causal relationship between patterns on the board.*

> *For example, say we want mathematical multiplication to emerge as a self organising emergence on the board. So that if we start with 3 cells in the left column and 4 cells on the first row the "game" will evolve into a pattern of 3x4 = 12 stable cells somewhere on the board. If we can create this behaviour so that when we start with N cells in the left column and M cells on the first row the game converge to NxM cells on the board we can model the program as implementing the mathematical formula NxM. But - what would be interesting is to compare this with the actual rules of each cell! From traditional playing around with **Conway's Game of Life we know we can get seemingly complicated patterns play out on the board although each cell applies very simple rules. So what simple rules self organise to "multiplication"?*

1) Can we imagine an alternative set of "natural laws" that can explain the observed behaviour of physical systems based on "cells" interacting and self organising into our observable universe?

> *The idea here is that if we can show that mathematically defined behaviour of matter and fields can also emerge from self organising "cells" of something else that emerge as the matter and fields we model the universe with, then we have in fact found an alternative way of modelling our physical reality. Now we would then need iterative and recursive computation to simulate reality (to simulate the stable state of the self organising system). But on the other hand we may potentially find new ways to interact with reality to "bend" it to our will?*

2) Consider that a model of the universe as an evolving self organising system may better allow for (or explain) why we find ourselves to exist in it and interact with it to figure out how to bend it to our will?

> *The deeply unsatisfactory conclusion of our current deterministic model of the universe is that it does not answer the questions we have of how to live a "good" life as a human being. For one, why would a deterministic universe come with the thing we call time if there is nothing of value that happens as time passes. What I mean is that if the outcome is already given by the initial condition, then the passing of time is just a nuisance. Time then is only there to separate the cause and effect but provides no ability for any computation to "choose" what will happen. And this observation of course is totally unsatisfactory to the experience any living thing has of the importance to actually figure and and choose how to survice and reproduce. If the determinism is at the base of anything that happens in the universe then why do we experience that we care, that truth is worth discovering and that wisdom and careful choice is the most important aspect of being alive?*
