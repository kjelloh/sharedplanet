1) Consider how things are designed to work by-themselves but very seldom to work-in- concert?

2) Consider how digital services are not designed to work in concert and all requires my personal data to reside with the service (and seldom allow me to separate the data from the service)?

==&gt; Consider how Final Cut Pro allows reference to personally stored and organised video and photos but prefer you “import” your data into libraries under control of the application?

==&gt; Consider how older application like Word, Excel originally totally separated your files from the control of the application?

==&gt; Consider how popular it is for vendors to design applications that require you to store your data in “the cloud” to be able to use the service?

==&gt; Consider how Fusion 360 require you to store and work with your data in the “cloud” provided by the vendor (and that your data becomes useless and potentially unreachable if you cancel the subscription)?

3) Consider how things are sold based on price and more or less synthetically measured “quality” or “performance” but never based on endurance or compose-ability?

==&gt; Consider how har it is to search the market for a thing that has the longest endurance whilst also stay compatible with the things you already have or will have in the future?

==&gt; Consider that part of the problem of inter-compatibility is the rate of progress (new things develops to become incompatible with older things)?

> *We can imagine that it is a price to pay for having progress that when things are developed to get “better” they also change so that they no longer work so well with older things you already have. And although that is true there seem to still be an incitament fro vendors of new things to deliberately make them not to work well with the things you have. Because then when you by new things you simultaneously get an incitement to replace and buy new the things you already have?*

4) Consider my computer build where I want to create a data storage decoupled from the computer and any device or service I use?

==&gt; Consider there seems to be no SD card reader designed to be built-in or hidden into a desktop table or workplace furniture?

> *Ideally I would like to buy an SD card reader that operated wirelessly on batteries and that I could mount behind by display, under the workspace table, at the armrests of my chair or anywhere where it is both out-of-the-way but still easily accessible when I need it?*

> *But all SD card readers I find if I search the web are ones that mounts with an USB cable to the computer and comes either as a dedicated SD card reader or as part of some form of USB docking port. And regardless of form factor they are all designed to either just hang out of the USB port or rest on the table besides the computer. In both cases being in the way and taking up space I would in 99% of the time use for other purposes.*

> *The observation here seems to be that those who design and sell SD card readers are only focused on making their product work stand-alone. No one tries to design a product that can “compose” with the things I already have?*

5) Consider how the digital world requires you as the user to link service together yourself manually?

==&gt; Consider how for my company I have to manually export reports from my book keeping web-based application into the web-interface of the Swedish tax agency?

> *When two digital service I use fails to interact correctly it becomes my problem to fix it. This happened recently when I finalised a quarterly report for VAT in my company. I used an application called FortKnox, a web based book keeping application. Not only was it somewhat hard to find the operation in the user interface to export the report to a format I may electronically send to the Swedish Tax Agency. For some reason the import of this report (to me as user it was a file I had to store locally on my computer as an intermediary) resulting in the Swedish Tax agency record it on the wrong period! One year earlier than the period I I was actually reporting on. This was Brought to my attention first several week later when the Swedish Tax Agency sent me an approval to change the report for the period one year ago. Which of course made no sense to me? After having suspected that some other company by misstake had made the erroneous report I learned that No, it was my report that had been miss-interpreted. Now, where is the error? Is it the FortKnox application that had generated erroneous data? And if so, was the erroneous data the result of something I did wrong? Or was ether something boring with the front end of the Swedish Tax Agency web service? I still don’t know. And further, I have very little information to my disposal to determine this. Still, it was I who had to refute the fee that the Swedish Tax Agency claimed I should pay for not having delivered the original report in time. You see, the error also caused me to fail to report for the quarter I was supposed to. The quarter I was sure I had reported!*
