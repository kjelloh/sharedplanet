As Alain de Botton argues, news are not reported in a form that makes sense or bothers us in the right way.

Suppose a news channel let novel writers invent fictive characters that ”experience" the news. It would be like reporting contemporary events in the same way as when describing history by writing a novel taking place in the time and environment one likes to describe.

1) People could become attached to these characters and thus care for what they come to face.  
2) One could focus on different aspects of described events by having different characters experience them (e.g. an israeli and palestine family experiencing the same event).  
3) One could make these news-operas run as reality-shows or soap-operas. But instead of inventing a story, one only has to invent how the characters react to real events!

I think this sounds like fun :). And it would certainly attract attention and discussion if it catches on and becomes large! And it can even be financed by commercials?

Now, some pitfalls may have to be addressed.

a) How does one avoid these news-channes to become channels for propaganda? What mechanisms can be used to stay faithful to the idea to convey the best picture of what is really going on?  
b) If financed by commercials, how does one avoid how the news are reported to be under the influence of commercial interests?  
c) On the other hand, news are already under influence of a lot of invisible interests, we just don’t see them. So maybe it is just a good thing to unveil this fact and have the news channel fight for trust instead?
