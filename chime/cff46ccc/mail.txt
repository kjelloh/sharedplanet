I experiences an interesting aspect of consciousness this morning between falling asleep and trying-to-wake-up.

1) I was falling asleep while listing to a pod-cast but the timer stopped the playback to soon - I wanted to listen a little bit longer. 

But I had already fallen asleep enough to not having the energy to pick up the phone and restart playback and setting the timer for another 10 minutes. I experienced how I decided to do it but I kind of only did it in my mind, almost like in a dream. Also, I was aware that my mind was going to sleep. My thoughts kind of wandered back and forth between wanting to pick up the phone and wanting to just let go and fall asleep. It was as if the conscious part of me that had decided to listen more come and went and was replaced in short intervals by a ”me” that experienced the dream more than ”reality”. My ”me” that wanted to listen more got at some point a little afraid and annoyed by lacking control, as if I was struggling with another person that fought me and made me forget want I was about to do.

2) What conclusions about the nature of consciousness can we draw from experiences like this? Does it support the theory about consciousness being created ”for us” to interact with our experiences?
3) In this falling-asleep state, was the brain in fact creating two consciousness at the same time. One that interacted with ”reality” and wanted to listen more to the pod-cast. And one that experienced my dreams and wanted to fall asleep?
4) Could we from this propose a model where an awake consciousness deals with experiences through the truth-web about external sensory inputs. And a dreaming consciousness deals with the scenarios presented to us through the truth-web of dreams?
