There seem to be an expected trait of a progressive idea or "Truth" that it can not make as much sense as its conservative protagonist just based on the "fact" that the conservative one will always be based on existing and proven observations while the progressive one being based on a wish for something better but yet to exist and yet to be proven "right".

> *So the idea here is to be fare about the problem of arguing for change to make "progress" at it will almost inevitably be considered "senseless" by those who care for how "things are" and has been proven through time to work.*

> *So we should expect the conservative view to be the one to make the most sense in that it will be able to give reason for keeping what exist and work simply based on everyone being able to make the same observations (as in observing things that is and has for a long time has existed) and argue for their continue existence based on the proposition that they will constinue to exist and work.*

> *We can see this way of reasoning by Jordan B. Peterson trying to save the "baby" in the bathwater of Christian stories as well as capitalistic and neoliberalistic arguments that free markets exists, works and seems correlated with growth of wealth and well being.*

> *On the other hand, Marxism has the problem of the opposite in that if you argue it has been tested then the test has failed. But if you argue it has not yet been tested (communism was not real marxism or socialism) then it is not observable to be evaluated nor is it proven to actually be applicable and have a good society emerge.*

> *So we should try to find a meta perspective on how to choose between conservative arguments and progressive ones in that we "know" we should progress to new ideas and way of having a good society emerge when there are reasons to do so. But that there is also always reasons to NOT change to protect valuable truths about what make a good society work based on proven and existing systems.*

> *It seems to me at the core of both these ways of arguing how to choose for the collective "good" van be modelled on the observation that we are all driven by what we "care" about. And by "caring" I mean the self evident emotion about the state of things and how the future should be. I argue that at the very least we should be frank about how the things we care about more or less just pops into existence as subjective evaluations inside each and every one of us. But that we should not expect them to be unchangeable. For one that they are the "most real" things we experience. But also they are the "most illusive" when it comes to their origin. And at least we should be able to all argue that we should search for the society where most of us get what we care for most. But that this may in fact in itself change as we make more observations about what others care about and trough discussion and experiments find out our common grounds to converge to a society we all care to participate in.*

> *It seems to me that what we care about is shaped by our observed experience. But also that our experience is shaped by what we care about. So imagining and arguing is only half of the process to find the solution. We must also allow for change to take place in some moderated fashion to allow for new ways of caring and new ways of experiencing reality.*
