So, I stayed up for the 01:30 to 04:30 Am, Sweden time, live stream of the "Dr. Jordan Peterson & Slavoj Žižek - Happiness: Capitalism vs Marxism", debate. And it was worth it!

1) Consider my takes on the debate as I remember it? (These are my comments on the debate as I remember it)

==&gt; Consider how good it felt to see Peterson seemingly "fall in love" with Zizek?

> *I made me so happy, to see Peterson fighting to not laugh out in joy and love for Zizek, as the debate went into its final phase; In the third hour or so. As a spectator I felt it, as if the strain seemed go away in Peterson posture, giving way for a relief that he an Zizek actually could to talk in respect, and somewhat playfully agree on quite a large number of issues. Peterson seemed to, a little embarrassedly, have to hold back something that looked like a combination of tears and love, to not burst out in a cute giggle or outright happy laughter. As if he wanted to give Zizek a warm friendly hug.*

> *It was warming and hopeful to see, and rimed very well with both speakers final notes, about what the audience should take home from the debate. Peterson - that this was a proof that two people with contradicting views, can sit down and discuss and learn in respect for one another. Zizek that hating was sloppy and that it is ok to think for yourself. Even if you are on the left you should not hate on those who you disagree with.*

==&gt; Consider Petersons and Zizeks half hour introductions totally failed to focus on the same thing?

> *Peterson used most of his opening half hour to to lay out ten crucial thesis he had selected from the Marx Communist Manifesto. And to criticise them in depth and length. In his usual way he mostly spoke without a script, only returning to his computer when necessary to read his notes. He seemed stressed and quite incoherent but made it through, several time though, based on pure routine it seemed. It was not quite convincing, a little predictable but at least felt like a show of courage, as he was on an arena with potential hostility in the audience towards him. There were some moments of misplaced laughters from the audience, which annoyed Petersson. And as a spectator at least, I felt the laughter was potentially* *signs of mocking and disapproval. As if Peterson has said something ridiculously stupid. And at one time he could to refrain from reacting with a "I suppose you would if you where one of those who just laughed" referencing something he had just said about how to NOT raise to power. But it was not clear if the laughters where perhaps only signs of nervousness in the audience. Perhaps those present at the event where affected by the tension of the open hostility Peterson was expressing against the Marxist doctrine?*

> *Zizek read from a script his whole half hour. And jumped from subject to subject in his typical Zizek-ian style. He mixed politics, comments on populism, capitalism, made some Freudian analysis of alienation and commodification of sex and pleasure, happiness, trump, some Peterson references to lobsters and so on. But it was quite interesting to listen to. And the take away from early on was that he did not refute the critique on Marxism as laid out by Peterson. And sometimes even acknowledged he agreed on most of the Peterson brought up points. Zizek stood out as being humorous but did also self identify as a deep pessimist. He was able to get several laughters from the audience during his half hour, that he, as he himself reflected on and admitted, overrun with almost 10 minutes. Which Peterson with a slight smile and a nod approved.*

==&gt; Consider how Petersson was surprised about what a strange marxist he now found Zizek to be?

> *Peterson expressed about half into the debate how strange a marxist he found Zizek to be. And he seemed delightful and curious about this in a sincere and almost affectionate way. He even asked Zizek why he insisted to be associated with, and extend the marxist line of thought, when in Peterssons assessment , Zizek seemed so distance himself from communism and marx and could very well, on his own merits, provide a line of thought totally a Zizek-ian philosophy? Peterson described Zizek to be an original and interesting thinker on his own rights. And praised Zizek to be attractive for how he presented himself and what he had to say.*

==&gt; Consider Zizek never explicitly praised Peterson but that he several times expressed respect and openness through inviting Peterson to play and tell jokes?

> *Zizek several times interrupted Peterson with short meta reflections on the debate itself, seemingly wanting to loosen up the atmosphere and invite Peterson to play? At least two times he made a joke about the moderator taking the role of Lenin to censor the question from the audience under the disguise of pretending the audience could ask any question they wanted. Peterson seemed to appreciate those little interruptions, but never lost his footing in the line of thought he was currently engaged in laying out.*

> *At one specific time Zizek interrupted and told a joke about how Niels Bohr anecdotally had hung a horse shoe above the entrance to his house. And when questioned why, based on him not believing in superstition, Bohr replied that - "Yes, but I have heard it works even if you did not believe in it!" To this everyone, including Peterson laughed openly, almost in relief. Then Zizek followed up with the meta- comment that he had told the joke to show that also Petersson was allowed to. Again, Zizek was, kind of metaphorically poking Peterson in the side to loosen up and play!*

==&gt; Consider how Zizek openly confronted Peterson why he had both defined and identified a post modern neo marxist as his antagonists?

> *The debate heated up a little when Zizek suddenly confronted Peterson about Post Modern Neo-marxism. Zizek said he failed to to see any such combination. He new marxists. He new post modernists, but could not understand how Peterson could explain anyone to be both? Now Peterson stood his ground. Although, for a short moment, I though me see he was a little shook. As if he realised that maybe he had gotten something wrong here. He took some time to gather composure and concentrate on what to say. Forming his hands in front of him as of trying to mould some truth out of thin air. He then carefully laid out his line of thought, how contemporary social justice movements, with a core of post modernity, had taken the pattern of "noble workers against villain bourgeoisie" from Marx class struggle model, and re-used it as "noble victimised vs privileged villain" in capitalist power structures.*

> *To this Zizek actually agreed. He also viewed the victimhood culture as violent and unproductive. But he in turn hold his ground that Marx actually had much better and true things to critique contemporary capitalism with, than that laid out in the Communist Manifesto. He mentioned some other titles by Marx I did not recognise, or had read myself, so we have to wait until May to listen to the recording again to hear what Zizek actually referred to here.*

> *Still I think also this interaction was a good one. They clarified their analysis of reality and their critique of the other. But also showed each other respect and appreciation and both declared several agreement on aspects of destructive reasoning on the left. *

> *And maybe, just maybe, Peterson, like me, was left with a realisation, that perhaps even in Marx thinking, there is a baby to save before throwing out the bathwater?*

==&gt; Consider Zizek in self-declaring as pessimistic and Petersson as promoting act-in-faith are not actually able to address the pain of modern society?

> *If we make the observation that the polarisation of the contemporary political arena, as mainly driven by an agreement that modernity hurts, but a disagreement on what ought to be done to make the pain go away, then neither Zizek nor Petersson actually has an overarching answer nor hope to provide? In this debate Zizek provided some all-over-the-place observations of pain driven destructive social movements combined with a black view on our ability to actually come up with a collective solution to drive the pain away. He agreed with Petersson that current social justice far left, is not a positive force for change, in that they fight only to gain self declared higher moral grounds, and aims at nothing else than the submission of "the powerful" to hand over privilege and back of from social influence. But then what? The far left just has no positive vision for the future. And Zizek has none either. But then Peterson has no better answer than a **Judeo-Christian value based free market capitalism, with all its flaws, but just better than any other system we have tried through human history, so far. Thus we may conclude that moderates, the conservatives nor far right has any better vision for the future than clinging on to faith in that which has worked in the past. But what if this time those no longer function?*

> *So in that sense none of these thinkers should actually be talking about a vision of a bright human future. They both preach a gospel that to their audiences sound like a recipe on how to adress the pain. Zizek seem to hope we find a way to survive the inevitable collapse. And Peterson hopes the collapse can somehow be avoided by individs picking up their cross and carry them uphill.*

> *I can't but feel that the answer somehow must be a synthesis of Zizek and Petersson call for action? There are babies, in the bathwaters of religion and enlightenment, as well as in the critique of modernity. We need to identify them, and agree on how to raise them as the base of a future society, that can protect and foster all human values, and provide meaningful lives for many generations to come. The pain is real! Change is inevitable. And not blame nor civil war is the way forward.*

> *In that sense the love that seemed to emerge between Zizek and Peterson may be just the spark to initiate the must required amalgamation of progress and conservatism?*
