I am interested in what seems to be a conflict between what we "feel" (as in experienced truth) and our conscious rational reasoning we "conclude" or "deduce".

The idea here is to view the value of rationality as an add-on to the subconscious feelings and self-evident experience. As such we may suspect that it is when what we feel or experience to be true is not actually the best truth, that rationality enables us to reason ourselves to a better truth. But if this is true then we should suspect that rationality is about questioning our subconscious expereinec rather than find a well grounded truth in the first place.

For one, it seems what we use as input in our rational reasoning is what our subconscious expose to us as a representation of reality. This means that the very starting point of any rational reasoning is what we have been handed from our unconscious "machinery". This means that we are able to rationally question what we "see" on our inner "screen". But we are NOT in the same way able to rationally conclude how reality actually "is" if what we experience is not to be trusted?

So maybe the value of rationality to detect when what we experience does not "make sense" so that we can go and explore. Because when we explore we can expose us to new information about reality that makes "more sense"?

But we can't escape that what we experience to be true comes from our subconscious process. We should expect we can't cover that part of "making sense" with our conscious rational add-on process?
