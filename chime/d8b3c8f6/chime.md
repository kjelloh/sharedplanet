> *So the basic observation here is that "plus" as in "this or this" for an abstraction of "counting things of the same type" requires time to be real and for free will to exist.*

> *We begin by observing that in our representation of our experienced reality we are able to create categories of things of the same type. From this follows we can reduce a gathering of things of the same type with the count of these things. By doing this abstraction we are able to represent the aspect of, let's call it "enough", of the things we have.*

> *From this follows we now also get the notion of "this or that". Because if we have two gatherings of things with the same count we can use gathering A or gathering B if all we care about is the count of the things. We also get the notion of "this or that" with the ability to count itself. Because what we do when we reduce the gathering of things to its count is that we no longer care about the order or the nature of each individual thing. From the gathering of things we can take "this or this or that..." as being the next thing from the gathering.*

> *So now we have "or" that gives us "plus" for counting. That is, if two things are "the same" and we can take "this or that", then from having count 1 of each, together we get a count of 2 of these things.*

> *But, for there to be a "this or that" in the first place we must have time to separate the states of "I have this" and "I have that". That is, at time t1 I have "this" and at some later time I have "that". Because, at any given time we only have "what is", we don not have "this or that" at the same time.*

> *Note: Here enters quantum superposition where in fact reality behaves exactly as if it is in "this or that" at the same time! This is super interesting and we should come back to this fact at some later time :)*

> *Anyhow, we now have made observations about how "this or that" enables us to represent reality using "count" from which follows that we need to separate state "I have this" from state "I have that" using time.*

> *Finally, the way we now navigate reality is to use our representation of reality to interact with it. And the interaction we perform is to "choose this" or "choose that". That is, from our point of view, we navigate reality by choosing how to mutate it given something can be "this" or "that".*

> *Note: This is true regardless wether reality actually is deterministic or not. That is, even if the universe as a whole, with us in it, is totally computable from its initial state to its end state - for us being only a part of reality we have to resort to "choosing" from the options we have in pour limited representation of reality.*

> *So so sum up, we represent reality as having things that are "the same" and from that follows these things can be interchanged as "this" or "that" being equal. This gives us the ability to model reality as "counts" of these things instead of each thing by itself. Finally we use this representation of reality to interact with it which means we have to "choose". And we measure our ability to "choose" according to how "free" we are to mutate it into becoming how we care for it to be. That is, we use our "free will" to "choose" how to mutate and navigate our representation of reality. *
