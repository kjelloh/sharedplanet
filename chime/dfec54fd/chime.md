Work may be divided into a) Work that is required to be done, b) Work that is allowed but not required and c) Work that is not allowed. Here ”Required to be done” means to accomplish the goal or needs of the society and its citizens. A company that needs work to be done to make a profit does not count unless the result of the work also serves the needs of the society.

Work may further be divided into 1) Work that few or no-one likes to do and 2) Work that many or all want to do. Here ”wants to do” should be from the point of view of ”not rewarding in itself”. One may argue that if rewarded enough any work can be made ”wanted”. Here we mean the work itself must be the reward. If the work in itself is not reward enough then the work is ”not wanted”.

Combing these two aspects we get.

a1, Required work that few wants to do.  
a2, Required work that many wants to do  
b1, Allowed work that few wants to do  
b2, Allowed work that many wants to do  
c1, Forbidden work that few wants to do  
c2, Forbidden work that many wants to do

Society should highly reward a1 and to lesser degree reward a2. It should punish (regulate) c2 and to a lesser degree also punish c1. Work of type b1 and b2 may be left unregulated.
