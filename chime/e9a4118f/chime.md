> A case for “Freedom is the gateway drug to hell”?

> This is a little “tunge in cheek” argument about the problems of our aim at getting as “free” as possible.

> Trying to focus on the problem of never questioning if - or why this is actually the best we can do?

> The way to really question something is to really steal-man an argument.

> In this case an argument about the problems of freedom.

> So lets try and do that?  
> -------

> We all like freedom, right?

> Nothing is better than freedom?

> When things are bad and need to be better - Aiming for freedom is an important step. Right?

> Well, the freedom of a well fed and un-threatened mind is to question even the most accepted truth - in search for signs of trouble!

> So, is freedom really so good we imagine it to be.

> And if not, what aspects of freedom, of allowance to act with as few restrictions as possible, can in any way be bad for us?

> (1) My first warning sign is that freedom is an illusion.

> There are aspects of the process of navigating through life that is in fact very restrictive.

> Some of them are obvious. We are not free to do things that get us killed.

> Now, you may say we should be free to do things that may potentially kill us.

> That, in fact, that is at the core of what maximised freedom should allow.

> And sure - but then ask yourself. What is worth dying for?

> Once you are dead you are out of options to act.

> Once dead your are basically not free to do anything.

> The paradox here is that freedom is reduced if you die.

> And, of course, if you kill others their freedom is also reduced.

> So, it seems we can at least make the case that if we want to maximise freedom we must actually reduce it.

> At least reduce it to such freedom that is maximum repeatable?

> (2) My second argument is that the more free we are, the less things are the right ones to do.

> If we are free to believe anything and do anything without judgement, then this also means less and less things are arguably the wrong things to believe or do.

> Although this may feel like it should mean that is is easier to do the right thing (more things are right)

> It actually means it is harder to do the right thing (so many things apart from what you are doing is also right)

> But the way we know we are doing the right thing is because it is not the wrong thing.

> And if less and less things are not wrong, we will get fewer and fewer clues to decide what is right.

> So the more free we allow ourselves to live, the more insecure about our path through life we will become.

> If anything goes, more and more things becomes of less and less significance.

> If doing the right thing takes no effort (because so much is the right thing and so much of the right things takes so little effort to pursue)

> Then taking risks and putting in a effort to explore what the right thing is - gets harder and harder.

> And any feelings we have reserved for the happiness to achieve something (the right thing) we have tried so hard to get, will get triggered less and less.

> (3) Thirdly, we live in concert with others. We navigate a landscape of human social interactions.

> We have to pick a path for ourselves. And it better be a path that works in concert with all others navigating their paths.

> Surely, we need a way to ensure that the paths we travel through life don’t conflict negatively with the paths of others?

> Because how free we ever imagine it is possible to be. We still have to share some space, some time and some company with others.

> But two bodies can not occupy the same location in space. We are not free to choose that.

> Two people can not breathe the same oxygen. We are not free to choose that.

> Two people can not drink the same cup of water. We are not free to choose that.

> Two people can not eat the same plate of food. We are not free to choose that.

> Two people can’t share the same relationships with others. We are not free to choose to share a brother, a mother or a partner.

> Placing freedom at the top of the list-of-things to strive for will fail in taking into account the things we simply can’t share or very few will want to share.

> And acting out freedom as the highest ideal - in friction with aspects of life that just are not, on the freedom market - in concert with billions of people - does produce a lot of disappointments, frustration and not least of all - confusion!

> Any hard earned wisdom from past generations on what worked, what was worth striving for and thus, what was the right thing to do, will be thrown away at the altar of freedom in this game.

> So while life remains the navigation problem it has always been. The free human is asked to navigate it without even the old map as a reference. And the more humans that navigates with new and experimental maps, the harder your own navigation will become.

> The most important and influential landscape humans navigate, are the social landscape. And the more each human draws their own maps. The less likely it is that an accurate map of of the social landscape actually exists!

> Freedom kills any wisdom that threatens it. It cares only for the right to not to care.

> Any - "Thao shalt not" - is the enemy of freedom.

> And Freedom will not even question it. It will simply disregard anything that resembles shackles and just throw them off.

> Freedom threatens to tear down any signs with potential directions to the good life.

> Freedom threatens to blind us from hard earned wisdom of past generations.

> Freedom may lead us to a place where those who comes after us is left with no freedom at all.

> That’s why freedom, maybe, is the gateway drug to hell!
