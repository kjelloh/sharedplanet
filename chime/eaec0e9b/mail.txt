> Dear friends, collaborators & passers-by we met whilst weilding mallets,
> 
> Over the last few months there have been some incredible accelerated developments on WikiHouse. There are now individuals and teams all over the place building, hacking and developing WikiHouse projects. Huge numbers of people have emailed us and joined the community groups. We've done our best to try to keep up, but many, many apologies for the times we've been slow, and thanks for your patience with our stretched time, resources and painfully limited collaboration platform.
> 
> So we thought it was about time we sent out a quick update on what WikiHouseUK team are working on and what's coming up. That includes finding funding, establishing and documenting the open organisational structure of WikiHouse as a global commons and developments in both hardware and software. We've also listed some key challenges others might be able to take on or contribute to.
> 
> Huge thanks and credit to all the people behind the project in London, to the teams who are making the project their own, to those supporting us, and particularly to all the people who are seeing in WikiHouse the opportunity to radically transform housing, debt, design, democracy and urban development. WikiHouse has the potential to be a huge game-changer, but its progress is marked as small, disruptive steps and milestones, which anyone can help take.
>  
> 
> We've released the WikiHouse Constitution
> 
> One of the practical challenges of open design is how to also developing open organisational models, to develop WikiHouse as a long-term global commons project, founded by us, but owned by everyone. We spend quite a lot of time thinking about new kinds of organisation for the new economy, and this is the result. We think there's some pretty radical stuff in there, but there are also some straightforward practical protocols for licensing and free collaboration. Rather unconventionally, it takes the form of a visual diagram. Perhaps most importantly, it includes the open trademark license for WikiHouse. If you're interested in setting up a WikiHouse chapter, it explains how you can do it. Click here to download it.
> 
> Like all first constitutions, it's not perfect. If you think there's something that should be changed, or you see something that for you would represent a barrier, do comment in the community group.
>  
> 
> 
> Building the first full house
> 
> After many prototypes and a few shelters, the race is on to complete the first fully-inhabited house, and to share the model, cutting files, specification and costings. A few weeks ago we released the v3.1 system, and we're working on a number of small projects to build the first full houses and prove that the system really works. Most excitingly, we're not the only ones, WikiHouseNZ, WikiHouseFR and FoundHouse are looking good to beat us to it.
> 
> 
> Improving the website
> 
> We are working on improvments to the WikiHouse website, a big part of which is improving the community tools. The Google groups we're currently using are better than nothing, but they're a pretty messy and ineffective way to share, and a pretty inaccessible way to connect. We're working on a better live collaboration / discussion space, to sit alongside a proper wiki for permanent content.
> 
> 
> Improving design software... towards parametric Wikihouse
> 
> As well as trying to find the resources to improve the WikiHouse SketchUp Ruby plugin, we've been plotting with the amazing guys at atMade on Jupiter who are behindSpoke Creator (Check out Evan Thomas' first shot at a WikiHouse Spoke > Browse spokes > Public > WikiHouse). Basically, Spoke is bringing closer something we thought was a long way away: an in-browser parametric design tool, that anyone can use to go from design-to-manufacture process in a few minutes. It is built on a grasshopper-like parametric workflow interface. Watch the tutorials here. The big challenge is this: To build the first full WikiHouse spoke of v3.1. If you, or someone you know is a ruby or grasshopper/parametric design jedi, and thinks they could give it a shot, let us know. It will be a game-changer.
> 
> How we're funding it
> 
> WikiHouseUK is still largely driven by volunteered time and investment from the team: 00, Espians and Momentum. We also have three open crowdfunding goals. They're not timed campaigns, we'll keep going until they are funded or completed, and let everyone know when they are. Huge thanks to everyone who has donated. Please do keep funding us and encouraging others to.
> 
> We're also hoping we'll soon be able to announce some seed funding for the WikiHouse platform.
> 
> Most of the hardware progress is driven by straightforward commissions, from individuals or organisations to design houses, structures or exhibition prototypes. 
> 
> WikiHouse is a non-profit project. If you know of any funds / foundations who would be interested in supporting the creation of an open source construction system in the commons, and the 'wikipedia of stuff', please shout, and send them the Open Challenge document.
> 
>  
> 
> 
> We're building a wiki
> 
> Another development that we are eager to have online is the Wiki, an online library where high-performance, low-threshold solutions for hardware products can be uploaded and shared accessibly. On the long run, this will include not just WikiHouses using CNC, but other structural systems, materials, products, and less tangible stuff like development models for different countries around the world...basically, you name it, all editable, documented, categorised and tagged. We often have community members asking how they can contribute, and the Wiki will be a key means for people to contribute and share their work and knowledge for others to use and improve.
> 
> Legals
> 
> When open software goes wrong, people lose time and money. When open hardware goes wrong, people lose limbs. Or worse. Legal liability and certification is a huge issue for all open hardware projects right now, and we're having some important conversations and getting some advice from some incredibly generous experts as to how we can establish a proper legal framework for the project.
> 
> Some open design challenges:
> 
> We get more offers and opportunities for collaboration than we can ever have time for. Which is fantastic. And frustrating. But there are a whole load of interesting open challenges which we think are fascinating and will be hugely important, and their things that anyone might be interested in taking on and solving, or might be already. So here's a quick ideas takeaway:
> 
> Build the WikiHouse Spoke
> Use Spoke creator to create a full parametric model of WikiHouse v3.1
> 
> Build a house
> Design and build a structure or complete house, model it in SketchUp and share it along with instructions, costings and photographs.
> 
> Windows
> We've already begun to develop WikiWindow, a high-energy target window unit which can be made for a fraction of the cost of market alternatives.That in itself could be a disruptive innovation. Do take a look, prototype and test it, improve it then share.
> 
> Kitchen
> Design a modular system of adaptable, standardised kitchen units. Think 'open source Ikea'.
> 
> Plugin
> Download the WikiHouse ruby extension for SketchUp from GitHub and improve it. It's still very buggy and doesn't export a CAD format. Could it: centralise part names and put them into a CNC-able font? Allow for varying material thicknesses? Allow for surface routing?...
> 
> Nesting
> Automated, efficient nesting of parts on cutting sheets is a huge challenge. If we can develop an open source script for doing it, it will have impact for makers far beyond the WikiHouse project.
> 
> Design and upload open products
> Install the WikiHouse plugin, then tinker, design and share solutions for house components that you think should be in the commons. e.g Furniture, Services, Arduino devices.
