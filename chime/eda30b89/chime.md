I want to understand the nature of laughter in response to events in reality and are following up on the suspicion that it is about some truth about reality we would otherwise be unable to accept?

> *So the idea here is to develop the basic proposition that laughter as an emotional response to experiences should make us suspect it is there for us to be rewarded to information we would benefit from paying attention to. Or put more shortly - we laugh when a valuable truth about reality is revealed to us.*

> *Now, why would we need this extra pleasurable emotion to find truth in addition no pain (avoid doing this) and curiosity (look for truth). And it struck me that maybe from the nature of jokes often having an element of the forbidden and often is about things that hurt and pose danger to us, that maybe jokes are ways to bridge from truths we do NOT want to see or trust into truth we should and can start to trust as true?*

> *For example, we know that we model the world through our model of reality. Without such a model the reality would not make sense to us. For there to exist dead and living things we need a model to categorise reality into these things. But we aslo know we resist models of reality that contradicts our own. We prefer our model of reality which shows up in behaviour we call "Cognitive Bias". Things we have learned we explain in our model of reality and interpret reality using this model the next time the same or similar thing happen.*

> *It makes sense that we trust and protect our model of reality. Each time we can show that our model outcompetes the models of others we have in practice shown our model to be "more correct" than other models. We talk about our model of reality to reveal "truth" and that we are "correct". So we "care" very much about being "correct". We "care" very much that our model of reality explains how we can interact with reality. So much so that we feel pain when our model of reality is threatened as in judged or questioned.*

> *Now, if we feel pain when presented with experiences and arguments that we may in fact model reality in a less "correct" way we would expect we have evolved a mechanism to still "change our minds" despite it being painful. So enter Jokes and laughter. When curiosity or plain experience of reality is not enough for us to adjust how we interact with it, we need to be pushed into change when change is called for. And I want to propose that jokes, humor and comedy are the information exchanging mechanisms in which new truths may be examined. In jokes we find alternative truths presented in an experimental "what if" context that we are rewarded with positive feelings to be able to take a good look at.*

> *A good example of this is Ricky Gervais roasting of Hollywood celebrities at the Golden Globe Awards 2020 (Youtube "**Ricky Gervais at the Golden Globes 2020 - All of his bits chained**" *  
> [*https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sR6UeVptzRg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sR6UeVptzRg)) where most of his jokes on the surface accused celebrities, social media tech companies for big flaws in their "wokeness" argumentation and political activism. But we make interpret the reason for this being "funny" is that in fact the things he said could very well refer to something being true that is not talked about. And at least from the support he got from the "other side" of wokeness culture at least they agreed. So the "truth" he "revealed" was that maybe if could be sound to listen to the alternative model of reality that is NOT part of wokeness. And the way he did that was by serving these alternative truths as jokes to be at least considered.*

> *But, the reason we laugh is NOT because what is revealed in the experience in actually true. We laugh because we are surprised and curious enough to take the experience under consideration. But in cases when the revealed truth is too far off from what we hold true, then we do NOT laugh. Then our internal "truth searching engine" is not triggered to even take the information under consideration as valid truth. In fact, the opposite emotion to laughter is pain in some form.*

> *In some respect it seems the emotion of disgust is the opposite of laughter. We show disgust when we encounter someone that acts in faith to a model of reality that you feel the need to resist and fight. When you "care" for some model of reality to be "false" or at least to trusted and acted upon you show and act in disgust.*
