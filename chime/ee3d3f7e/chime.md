Once we define Deep Richness as a measure of wealth in terms of all values that matters to life, we may start discussing the best way to invest money to acquire it.

1) simply having a lot of money only partly contributes to deep richness (freedom to choose and enjoy free time and activities only for pleasure)  
2) Society mostly leaves us clueless on what we should do with money to really make our lives "rich"  
3) Money tries, but fails, to measure different deep wealth values in the same (the monetary) scale. But monetary value only measures how much we want it, not how deeply wealthy we will become!  
4) Humans were able to live quite happily also before we invented money. Thus we have to realise that money is something we transact in return for value. Acquiring money may in itself provide a deep value, e.g. Of not having to worry about food, sickness or old age. But we may also spend it to provide food, healthcare and pension in return for having it been paid for when we ourselves need it.
