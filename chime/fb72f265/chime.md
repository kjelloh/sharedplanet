2) Consider what it is that makes me find Sam Harris argument against Free Will so "boring", naively true and still Irrelevant?

> *Who am I to say Sam Harris just does not get to the bottom of Free Will? That what he is saying is in some sense naively true. But at the same time almost entirely misses the point.*

> *For one, to me Free Will does not mean I can choose what I find liking or thinking. Perhaps this is news to some people that you do not think your thoughts or will your will. But most people agree that we have an unconscious part that does most of the work. And our job is to listen to what comes out of our unconsciousness and somehow relate to it. In that sense Sam Harris is kind of arguing for the fact that how we experience reality is an unconscious process. What our unconscious serves us is out of our control when it happens.*

> *But secondly, question is if we are able to run some "program" on this reality that at some later time makes us want something else or do something that is NOT what the first impulse tells us to do?*

> *For what I can tell the door is still open to call our conscious processing our "Free Will" in that it is free to question if what we feel like doing is actually the thing to do?*

> *Even if we restrict also the conscious processing of alternatives to take place in a totally deterministic universe there may still be some correlation between our experience that we "try to decide" and what we end up doing? Like, if we do not consciously "choose" we end up acting on our first unconsciously produced impulse?*

> *Thirdly, Sam seems to totally focus on our inability to "cause" what we find ourselves wanting and forget that what we consciously do with this information may be to "decide" how to act in the future should a simmilar situation come up. My experience tells me that what I consciously find reasonable can make me act so that what I want in the future actually DOES change.*

> *In this sense the thing we call Free Will is our conscious experience of "deciding" how to relate to "reality" to shape what our unconscious processes tells us to do in the future.*

> *And this Free Will can very well be operating in a totally deterministic universe just as a computer program does. It still "decides".*

> *Finally, I want to investigate what we can say about life being a separated part of reality, interacting with reality (navigating) based on a a model of it enabling it to "be about" reality. Such a living being would be totally deterministic from the universe point of view but still unable to be deterministic to itself or other living beings. It would have to "decide" based on incomplete information processed through its model of reality. And this to me fits very well as having a "will", and a "Free" one at that (from its own perspective)?*
