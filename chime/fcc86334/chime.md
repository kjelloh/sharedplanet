==&gt; Consider this article that seems to indicate we can make a paste of raw egg and grind coffee to enhance coffee particle separation (<https://recipes.howstuffworks.com/egg-your-coffee-some-say-its-the-best-way.htm>)?

> “ the egg in this instance isn't used as part of the beverage; it's used as a filter and clarifying agent during the brewing process.”

> “Method:
1. Boil 1 cup of water for every one cup of coffee desired. For instance, for 3 cups of coffee, boil 3 cups of water.
2. Measure 1 tablespoon of coarsely ground coffee per 1 cup of boiling water.
3. Mix coffee grounds, egg and 3 tablespoons cold water into a paste.
4. Add the coffee-egg mixture to the boiling water, stir well and turn down the heat.
5. Simmer for 3 minutes, and remove from heat.
6. Add 1 cup cold water. The coffee grounds will continue to bind with the egg, and will sink to the bottom.
7. Strain or ladle the liquid into individual cups or a large vessel."



==&gt; It seems the article refers to the effect of having egg mixed in with the coffee is in fact flocculation (as we wanted)?

> “ the particles of coffee grounds and egg clump together in a process known as flocculation"
