We need some argument why it is better to adjust our belief to what is closer to truth than that what ”has always been true”.

Can we actually say that a belief that contradicts observations may not still be the best for us?

True that development in science, technology and society has been beneficial to us. But can we actually be sure this is because what we have reasoned and discovered about the truth about ourselves and the world is what has given us this prosperity. Can we even say that the things we have discovered has anything with reality-as-it-is?

Maybe our models of the world, the laws of physics, are in fact not true. They just work good enough and we are able to take advantage of this. Maybe it is the benefits these discovery has given us that is why we think they are true. But we do not actually care if they are true or not?

As Donald Hoffman argues in his TED talk our brain creates a desktop to reality, a user interface, that best suits us. He shows us that variants of creatures that sees reality as-it-is always dies it in evolution combat with creatures that has a best-fitted user interface.

From this we may argue that we may not actually benefit from understanding reality as-it-is (the true reality) but actually should develop a ”truth” of reality that suits us?

This question is important as it is in the core of if religious beliefs is actually inferior to scientific or atheistic non-beliving. It could be that we are actually better of to go for some form of ”untrue” beliefs about the world if this enables to live better and richer lives (whatever that means).

Unger talks about wishful thinking and that this must be avoided. Why? Where is the argument that humanity may not in fact find the best and fullest life by believing in something that is not fully true?
